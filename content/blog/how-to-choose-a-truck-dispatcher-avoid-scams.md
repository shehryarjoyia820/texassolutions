Knowing **how to choose a truck dispatcher** can save you money, time and stress. Most dispatchers are honest people doing hard work. But truck dispatch service scams exist, and new owner-operators are common targets. This guide covers the red flags, the questions to ask a truck dispatcher, how to check reviews, and what belongs in a dispatch service agreement.

## Common truck dispatch service scams and red flags

Watch for these warning signs:

- **Large upfront fees.** Paying hundreds of dollars before a single load is booked is a risk. A dispatcher earns money by booking loads.
- **Guaranteed income.** No one can guarantee loads, rates or weekly earnings. Freight markets change every day.
- **"Haul under our MC."** If someone asks you to run loads under their authority, or wants to re-broker your freight without broker authority, walk away.
- **Forced dispatch.** You should be able to say no to any load.
- **No written agreement.** Verbal deals lead to disputes.
- **Payments routed to the dispatcher.** Broker payments should go to you or your factoring company, not to the dispatcher's account.
- **Pressure tactics.** "Sign today or lose the spot" is a sales trick, not a business term.
- **Contact details that do not match.** A website, phone number and email that lead to different names is a warning.

Know the basic difference between a dispatcher and a broker. A dispatcher works for the carrier. A broker arranges freight between shippers and carriers and needs its own broker authority. See [truck dispatcher vs freight broker](/blog/truck-dispatcher-vs-freight-broker.html).

## What legit truck dispatch companies have in common

Legit truck dispatch companies usually share these traits:

- A clear website with fees, services and contact details
- A real business address and a phone number that someone answers
- A written agreement you can read before signing
- Loads booked under your own authority
- You approve every load before it is booked
- Honest answers when you ask what they cannot do

## Questions to ask a truck dispatcher

Ask these before you sign anything:

1. **How do you charge?** Percentage of gross, flat weekly fee or per load?
2. **What is the fee based on?** Linehaul only, or gross including extras?
3. **Is there a setup fee, monthly fee or contract term?**
4. **Do I approve every load?**
5. **Who signs rate confirmations?**
6. **Which equipment and lanes do you know?**
7. **Do you work with new authority?**
8. **How do you handle detention, lumper and TONU?**
9. **Can I speak to carriers you dispatch now?**
10. **How do I end the agreement?**

## Worked example: comparing a flat fee and a percentage fee

Say one dispatcher charges a **flat $300 a week** and another charges **5% of weekly gross**.

- At **$5,000** gross: 5% = $250. The percentage is cheaper by $50.
- At **$8,000** gross: 5% = $400. The flat fee is cheaper by $100.
- Break-even: $300 / 0.05 = **$6,000** of weekly gross.
- In a week with **no loads**: the flat fee is still $300. A percentage of zero is zero.

Neither is always better. Know your typical weekly gross, and ask what you pay in slow weeks. Run your own numbers in the [dispatch fee calculator](/estimate.html).

## What should be in a dispatch service agreement

A good dispatch service contract is short and clear. It should include:

- **The fee** and exactly what it is calculated on
- **Services included,** such as load search, negotiation, setups and billing
- **Load approval:** the carrier approves every load
- **Authority limits:** what the dispatcher may sign or submit for you
- **Payment flow:** brokers pay the carrier or the factoring company
- **When fees are billed** and how they are paid
- **Termination:** how either side can end it, and any notice period
- **Documents and data:** how your MC, insurance and banking details are stored and used

Read every line. If something is unclear, ask for it in writing. If a term would trap you, do not sign.

## How to check dispatch service reviews

Searching for the best dispatch companies reviews is a good start, but reviews need care:

- **Use several sources.** Search engines, map listings, trucking forums and social media groups. Do not rely only on the dispatcher's own site.
- **Read the details.** Useful reviews mention lanes, equipment and how problems were handled.
- **Watch for patterns.** Many short five-star reviews posted in the same week can be a warning sign.
- **Read the bad reviews.** See how the company responded.
- **Ask for references.** Call two or three carriers they dispatch now.
- **Test with one load.** How a dispatcher handles your first load tells you a lot.

## Who are the most trusted truck dispatch companies?

There is no official list. Any "top 10" list may be paid advertising. The most trusted dispatcher for you is one that is open about fees, puts terms in writing, lets you approve every load, and answers your questions directly.
