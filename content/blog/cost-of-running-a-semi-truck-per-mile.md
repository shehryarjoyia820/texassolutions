Before you accept any load, you need one number: **what it costs you to move your truck one mile.** Everything else, from rate negotiation to choosing lanes, depends on it.

## The industry benchmark

The American Transportation Research Institute's *Operational Costs of Trucking* study found the industry-average cost to operate a truck in **2025 was $2.336 per mile**, up 3.4% from the previous year and the highest in the report's history. Excluding fuel, the average was **$1.854 per mile**. Costs rose in every major category, with the biggest percentage jumps in tolls, repair and maintenance, driver benefits and tires.

Treat that as a benchmark, not your number. It covers fleets with company drivers and varying equipment. Yours depends on your own truck and lanes.

## Fixed vs variable costs

| Fixed (per month) | Variable (per mile) |
|---|---|
| Truck and trailer payment | Fuel |
| Insurance | Maintenance and repairs |
| Permits, licensing, ELD | Tires |
| Accounting, software, misc. | Tolls, and driver pay if you employ drivers |

**Fixed cost per mile** = monthly fixed costs divided by miles driven. It falls as you run more miles.
**Variable cost per mile** = fuel + maintenance + tires + other per-mile costs.
**Cost per mile (CPM)** = fixed per mile + variable per mile.

## Worked example

Say your fixed costs are **$2,650 a month** and you run **9,000 miles**. Fixed cost is about $0.29 per mile. Add fuel at $0.65, maintenance and tires at $0.18, and driver pay or your own pay target at $0.55, for $1.38 variable. Your all-in cost is roughly **$1.67 per mile**.

Plug in your own numbers with the [cost per mile calculator](/cost-per-mile-calculator.html). Fuel is the most volatile line: see [today's diesel price in your state](/diesel-prices/texas.html) and estimate trip fuel with the [fuel cost calculator](/truck-fuel-cost-calculator.html).

## From cost per mile to a rate you can accept

1. Work out your CPM.
2. Add your profit target per mile.
3. Compare against the load's **rate per total mile**, including deadhead, not just per loaded mile.

The [load profitability calculator](/load-profitability-calculator.html) does this for any load, and the [break-even calculator](/break-even-calculator.html) shows the miles you need each month to cover your costs.

## Ways to bring your cost per mile down

- **Cut deadhead.** Empty miles burn fuel with no revenue. Plan your next load before this one delivers.
- **Buy diesel smart.** Prices can differ by more than $2 a gallon between states.
- **Stay ahead of maintenance.** A repair reserve avoids a surprise bill; use the [maintenance budget calculator](/maintenance-budget-calculator.html).
- **Run enough miles** to spread fixed costs, but only on loads that clear your CPM.

A dispatcher can help with the first and last: planning lanes to reduce empty miles and negotiating rates against your real numbers. [See what dispatch costs](/estimate.html).
