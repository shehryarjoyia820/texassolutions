**Expedited freight** is the part of trucking where time matters more than anything else. A factory line is down waiting for a part, a hospital needs supplies tonight, or a shipment missed its original truck. The shipper pays for a dedicated vehicle that goes straight to the receiver, and the carrier that answers fast and delivers on time gets the next call.

## What makes freight "expedited"

Expedited freight usually has these traits:

- **Time-critical delivery.** Often same-day, overnight or a guaranteed appointment.
- **Exclusive use.** The vehicle carries only that shipment, with no other pickups or drops on the way.
- **Short notice.** Loads can come in with an hour or less before pickup.
- **Direct routing.** The driver goes from pickup to delivery with only legal breaks and fuel stops.
- **Close tracking.** Shippers and brokers expect regular updates and fast proof of delivery.

The freight itself can be small, like a box of parts, or a full truckload. What makes it expedited is the service, not the size.

## Vehicle types in expedite

| Vehicle | Typical freight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cargo or sprinter van | Small, light shipments | Lowest cost, lots of competition |
| Straight truck (box truck) | Several pallets, often with liftgate | Popular for regional expedite |
| Hotshot (pickup plus trailer) | Machinery parts, oilfield, construction | Often flatbed or gooseneck |
| Semi with team drivers | Full loads over long distances | Team keeps the truck moving around the clock |

Many expedite carriers equip vehicles with E-track, load bars, pallet jacks and sometimes temperature control. Some customers also ask for drivers with hazmat endorsements, TWIC cards or the ability to cross into Canada, since those widen the freight you can take. ## How expedited rates work

Expedite is usually priced per mile or as a flat rate for the run, with the premium reflecting speed and exclusive use. Things that raise the rate:

- Very short notice
- Tight or overnight delivery windows
- Team service
- Special requirements (liftgate, temperature, hazmat, security)
- Weekend and holiday runs

The catch is that expedite can be lumpy. You might run hard for three days, then sit waiting for the next load. Some carriers wait close to freight-heavy areas so they can respond fast when a call comes in. That waiting time is usually unpaid.

### Hypothetical weekly example

A straight truck runs three expedited loads in a week:

- Load 1: 450 miles at $2.60/mile = $1,170
- Load 2: 300 miles at $2.80/mile = $840
- Load 3: 620 miles at $2.50/mile = $1,550
- Total revenue: **$3,560** on 1,370 loaded miles
- Empty repositioning between loads: 400 miles
- Effective rate per total mile: $3,560 / 1,770 = **about $2.01**

That is still decent, but it is a lot lower than the rate on any single load. Check your own numbers with the [deadhead miles calculator](/deadhead-miles-calculator.html).

## Hours of service and team driving

Expedite tempts drivers to push, but hours-of-service rules still apply to vehicles covered by them. A solo driver in a covered vehicle has limits on daily driving and on-duty time, plus weekly limits. When a delivery is too far to reach legally solo, the answer is a team, not a falsified log. Learn the limits in our [hours of service guide](/blog/hours-of-service-rules-explained-11-14-70-hour.html), and if you are thinking of running with a partner, read the [team driving guide](/blog/team-driving-guide.html). Always confirm current rules with FMCSA.

## What it takes to succeed in expedite

1. **Be reachable.** Many loads go to the first qualified carrier who answers. Missing calls means missing freight.
2. **Be honest about ETAs.** Give a realistic pickup and delivery time, then beat it. A missed promise on a critical load loses the customer.
3. **Keep the vehicle ready.** Fuel up, keep equipment stocked, and stay ahead on maintenance. A breakdown on an expedited load is a disaster for everyone.
4. **Communicate constantly.** Send pickup confirmation, regular location updates and proof of delivery right away.
5. **Position smartly.** Learn which areas generate expedited freight and where you can get reloaded.
6. **Keep paperwork clean.** Current insurance, authority and carrier packets let you get set up with new brokers quickly.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- Higher pay per loaded mile than standard freight in many cases
- Often quicker loading and unloading, since the freight is urgent for the receiver too
- Strong repeat business once customers trust you

**Cons**

- Irregular schedule and unpaid waiting time
- High pressure on every load
- More empty miles repositioning
- Little room for mechanical problems

## Is expedite right for you?

Expedite fits drivers who like to move fast, stay on the phone and do not mind an unpredictable schedule. If you want a regular home time and the same lanes every week, dedicated or regional freight may suit you better. Many carriers mix both, taking expedited loads when they pay well and standard freight to fill the gaps.
