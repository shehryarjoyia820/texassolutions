**Trucking KPIs** (key performance indicators) are the handful of numbers that tell you whether your truck is making money and why. Gross revenue is not enough: a truck can gross a lot and still lose money if deadhead, fuel or slow-paying brokers eat the margin. Here are ten KPIs worth tracking, how to calculate each one, and a hypothetical example that ties them together.

## Why KPIs matter more than gross revenue

Gross is what everyone talks about, but profit decides whether you stay in business. KPIs let you:

- Compare loads and lanes on equal terms
- Spot rising costs before they become a crisis
- Negotiate with confidence because you know your floor
- Decide when you are ready to add a truck

## The 10 KPIs to track

### 1. Revenue per total mile
Total revenue divided by **all** miles driven, loaded and empty. This is your real rate, not the per-loaded-mile figure on the rate confirmation.

### 2. Cost per mile
All costs (fixed and variable) divided by total miles. Calculate it monthly with the [cost per mile calculator](/cost-per-mile-calculator.html). Our guide to the [cost of running a semi truck per mile](/blog/cost-of-running-a-semi-truck-per-mile.html) explains what goes in it.

### 3. Profit per mile
Revenue per total mile minus cost per mile. If this number is thin or negative, more miles will not fix it.

### 4. Deadhead percentage
Empty miles divided by total miles, times 100. Lower is generally better, though a short deadhead into a strong market can pay off. See [what deadhead is and how to reduce it](/blog/what-is-deadhead-in-trucking.html).

### 5. Miles per truck per week
How much the truck actually works. Low miles may point to long waits, poor load planning or too much home time for your cost structure.

### 6. Revenue per truck per week
A simple utilization check that matters a lot once you have more than one truck.

### 7. Fuel economy (MPG)
Gallons used versus miles driven. Track it per truck and per driver; changes often signal mechanical issues or driving habits.

### 8. Maintenance cost per mile
Repairs, tires and maintenance divided by miles. A steady rise usually means a truck is getting closer to a major repair.

### 9. Average days to pay
How long, on average, from delivery to cash in the bank. Slow payment strains cash flow even when you are profitable.

### 10. Unpaid time
Hours spent in detention, waiting on loads, or broken down. Track detention hours and whether they were paid, so you know which shippers and brokers cost you time.

## Formulas at a glance

| KPI | Formula |
|---|---|
| Revenue per total mile | Total revenue / (loaded miles + empty miles) |
| Cost per mile | Total costs / total miles |
| Profit per mile | Revenue per total mile - cost per mile |
| Deadhead % | Empty miles / total miles x 100 |
| Miles per truck per week | Total miles / trucks / weeks |
| Fuel economy | Total miles / gallons used |
| Maintenance cost per mile | Maintenance and repair costs / total miles |
| Average days to pay | Sum of (payment date - delivery date) / number of loads |

## Worked example: one hypothetical month

Here is a **hypothetical** month for one truck:

- Revenue: $22,000
- Loaded miles: 8,400
- Empty miles: 1,100
- Total costs: $17,575
- Gallons used: 1,520
- Maintenance and repairs: $1,330

Now the KPIs:

- **Total miles:** 8,400 + 1,100 = 9,500
- **Revenue per loaded mile:** $22,000 / 8,400 = about $2.62
- **Revenue per total mile:** $22,000 / 9,500 = about **$2.32**
- **Cost per mile:** $17,575 / 9,500 = **$1.85**
- **Profit per mile:** $2.32 - $1.85 = about **$0.47**
- **Deadhead:** 1,100 / 9,500 = about **11.6%**
- **Fuel economy:** 9,500 / 1,520 = **6.25 mpg**
- **Maintenance per mile:** $1,330 / 9,500 = **$0.14**

The rate confirmations averaged $2.62 per loaded mile, but the business really earned $2.32 per mile driven. That gap is the cost of deadhead. Cutting empty miles from 1,100 to 600 would raise revenue per total mile with no change in rates.

## How to track KPIs without a big system

1. **Keep a load log.** One row per load: date, broker, lane, loaded miles, empty miles, rate, accessorials, delivery date and payment date.
2. **Keep a monthly expense summary** using the same categories every month.
3. **Calculate weekly** the load-level numbers: revenue per total mile, deadhead and miles.
4. **Calculate monthly** the full set, including cost per mile and maintenance per mile.
5. **Compare to last month and the same month last year.** Trends matter more than a single number.
6. **Act on one number at a time.** Pick the weakest KPI and work on it for a month.

## Using KPIs to make decisions

- **Accepting a load:** check revenue per total mile including the deadhead to reach it, against your cost per mile.
- **Dropping a broker:** if their average days to pay keeps climbing, factor that into whether their rates are worth it.
- **Choosing lanes:** compare profit per mile by lane, not by load.
- **Adding a truck:** only when the first truck has steady profit per mile, healthy reserves and you understand what a second truck changes. Read [how to grow from 1 to 5 trucks](/blog/grow-from-1-to-5-trucks.html).
