A phone can run much of a one-truck business, but only if the right tools are on it. With so many **trucking apps for owner-operators** competing for your money, it helps to think in categories: what job does each app do, and do you really need it? This guide covers the main types, what to look for, and the questions to ask before you subscribe.

We do not recommend specific brands here. Features and prices change often, so compare current options yourself against the checklists below.

## Core apps most owner-operators need

### 1. Truck-specific GPS and navigation

A truck GPS lets you enter height, weight, length, axle count and hazmat status, then routes around low bridges, weight-restricted roads and truck-prohibited routes.

Look for:

- Vehicle profile settings for every truck and trailer you run.
- Up-to-date truck restrictions and live traffic.
- Offline maps for areas with weak signal.
- Points of interest such as truck stops, scales and rest areas.

Even with a good truck GPS, check posted signs and your company's route plan. No app is perfect.

### 2. ELD and hours of service

If you are required to use an electronic logging device, the system must be on FMCSA's registered ELD list. Most ELDs come with a driver app for logs and inspections.

Look for:

- Clear hours of service clocks and alerts.
- Easy roadside data transfer for inspectors.
- Electronic DVIRs.
- Mileage by state exports for IFTA.
- Good support when the device malfunctions.

Confirm current ELD rules and the registered device list on the FMCSA website. To double-check a trip plan, our [hours of service calculator](/hours-of-service-calculator.html) is a quick reference.

### 3. Load boards

Load board apps let you search and book freight, set lane alerts and check market conditions. Compare features such as search filters, rate data, broker credit and payment information, and how many loads are posted on your lanes. Our [load boards guide](/blog/truck-loads-and-load-boards-guide.html) explains how they work.

### 4. Document scanning

Brokers and factoring companies want clean copies of bills of lading, proof of delivery and receipts, fast. A scanning app turns phone photos into readable PDFs.

Look for:

- Automatic edge detection and contrast cleanup.
- Multi-page documents in one file.
- Easy sending by email or upload.
- Cloud backup so documents are not lost with the phone.

### 5. Expense and mileage tracking

You need records for taxes, IFTA and knowing your real cost per mile. An expense app or simple accounting tool can:

- Photograph and categorize receipts.
- Track fuel gallons and cost by state.
- Track income by load.
- Export reports for your CPA.

Talk to a CPA about which records you need to keep and for how long. Pair your records with the [cost per mile calculator](/cost-per-mile-calculator.html) to see what each mile costs.

## Useful extras

| App type | What it helps with | Worth it if you... |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel price finder | Compares diesel prices along your route | Fuel in many states or buy fuel at many stops |
| Fuel card app | Discounts, spending controls, receipts | Use a fuel card that offers one |
| Truck parking | Shows lots and, in some cases, available spaces | Run nights or busy corridors |
| Weigh station bypass | May let you bypass open scales if eligible | Pass many scales on your regular lanes |
| Weather and road conditions | Storms, wind, chain laws, closures | Run mountains, plains or winter routes |
| Factoring app | Submit invoices and track payments | Factor your receivables |
| Communication | Messaging with dispatch, brokers and shippers | Juggle many contacts each day |

## Questions to ask before you pay for any app

1. **What problem does it solve for me?** If you cannot name one, skip it.
2. **What does it really cost?** Check monthly fees, setup charges, per-truck pricing, hardware and cancellation terms.
3. **Is there a contract?** Avoid long commitments until you know the app works for you.
4. **Does it work with my other tools?** Exports to spreadsheets or accounting software save hours.
5. **Does it work offline?** Many lanes have dead zones.
6. **Who owns my data?** Read how the app uses and shares your location, load and financial data.
7. **What does support look like?** Ask whether help is available nights and weekends when you are actually driving.
8. **Is it secure?** Use strong passwords, two-step login where offered, and never share login details through texts or emails you did not expect.

## Watch out for scams

Fraudsters target carriers through fake apps, phishing links and fake load offers. Download apps only from official app stores, check the publisher, and be careful with any app that asks for broad access to your accounts. Never give your login, FMCSA portal details or bank information to someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

## Example: a lean app setup (hypothetical)

A new one-truck owner-operator might start with:

- One truck GPS with offline maps.
- One registered ELD with IFTA mileage export.
- One load board subscription.
- One scanning app with cloud backup.
- One expense tracker or spreadsheet shared with a CPA.

Extras like a fuel price finder or parking app can wait until you see where you lose time or money on the road. If each paid tool costs a modest monthly fee, add them up and include the total in your fixed costs. Review the list every few months and cancel anything you have not used.
