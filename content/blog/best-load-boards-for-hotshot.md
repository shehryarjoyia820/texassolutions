Hotshot freight moves fast. A load posted at 7 a.m. may be gone by 7:15, and the hotshot who gets it is usually the one who already knew the load would fit, had the broker's setup done and called first.

This guide explains how to pick a hotshot load board, how to search for freight your setup can actually haul, and what to do when the boards are slow. For a broader comparison across equipment types, read the [best load boards for owner-operators](/blog/best-load-boards-for-owner-operators.html).

## What to look for in a hotshot load board

Compare boards on what matters to a pickup and gooseneck, not on total load counts:

- **Hotshot and partial flatbed volume in your region.** A board full of full-size flatbed loads does not help if you can only haul a fraction of that weight.
- **Filters for length and weight.** You should be able to filter loads by size so you only see what fits.
- **Broker credit and payment data.** Slow or non-paying brokers can sink a small operation.
- **Rate history.** Knowing what a lane paid recently helps you negotiate.
- **Phone app.** You will be searching from truck stops and job sites.
- **Price against bookings.** If a board does not produce booked loads, it is an expense, not a tool.

## Load boards hotshot operators commonly compare

These are public load boards that many carriers use. We name them so you know where to start, not to endorse them. Features and pricing change, so check each one directly.

### DAT

A large US load board with equipment filters and, depending on plan, broker and rate information.

### Truckstop

A large board with load search, broker credit details and rate tools at different plan levels.

### 123Loadboard

A board used by many smaller carriers, with load search and broker details depending on plan.

### Broker carrier portals

Large brokers often post open-deck freight on their own carrier apps or websites. Once you are set up with them, those portals can be a steady source of loads.

## How to find hotshot loads on a board

### Know your numbers before you search

Write these on a card or save them in your phone:

- Deck length and usable deck space.
- Maximum legal payload, from your truck and trailer ratings and your license class.
- Equipment on hand: straps, chains, binders, tarps, ramps.

If you are running a non-CDL setup, your payload may be much lower than a CDL hotshot. See [non-CDL box truck and hotshot loads](/blog/non-cdl-box-truck-and-hotshot-loads.html).

### Set up smart searches

- **Equipment:** hotshot first, then flatbed and step deck partials.
- **Origin:** your current city with a deadhead radius. Start around 75 to 150 miles; hotshot freight is often more spread out than van freight.
- **Weight and length:** at or below your real limits.
- **Saved searches and alerts:** let the board notify you so you can call faster.

### Ask the right questions on the call

- Exact weight, dimensions and number of pieces.
- How it loads and unloads: forklift, crane, ramps, or by hand.
- Securement and tarp requirements.
- Pickup and delivery windows, and whether the receiver is a jobsite.
- Detention terms and how quickly the broker pays.

## Check the math, including deadhead

Example with assumed figures:

- Load pays **$1,350** for 300 loaded miles
- Empty miles to pickup: 90
- Your all-in cost per mile (assumption): **$2.10**
- Cost: (300 + 90) x $2.10 = **$819**
- Profit: $1,350 - $819 = **$531**, before dispatch or factoring fees
- Posted rate: $1,350 / 300 = $4.50 per loaded mile
- Effective rate per mile driven: $1,350 / 390 = **$3.46**

Also think about where the load leaves you. A strong rate into an area with no outbound freight can cost you the next day. ## Hot shot loads near me: staying regional

If you want to stay close to home:

- Search from your home base with a tight radius and sort by newest postings.
- Track which brokers post in your area repeatedly and build a relationship with them.
- Look at local industries. Construction, agriculture, equipment dealers and manufacturing all ship small open-deck freight.
- After every delivery, ask the broker if they have anything heading back toward your area.

## When the boards are not enough

New hotshot authorities often run into the same problems: brokers who will not set up a new MC, loads that vanish before you can call, and rates that barely cover fuel. Options beyond the boards:

- **Direct broker relationships** built through reliable service.
- **Direct shipper work** with local businesses that move equipment or materials.
- **A hotshot dispatcher** who already has broker contacts and can handle setup packets for you.

## Load board or dispatcher?

- **A load board** shows you postings. You still search, call, negotiate and handle paperwork.
- **A dispatcher** does that work and uses boards along with broker contacts.
