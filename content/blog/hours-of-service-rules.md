Hours-of-service (HOS) rules keep tired drivers off the road, and they also set how many miles a truck can legally run in a day and week. Here is a plain-English summary for **property-carrying drivers** under FMCSA rules (49 CFR Part 395). Always confirm current rules and your own exemptions with FMCSA and your ELD provider.

## The core limits

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| **11-hour driving limit** | You may drive a maximum of 11 hours after 10 consecutive hours off duty. |
| **14-hour on-duty window** | You may not drive after the 14th consecutive hour after coming on duty. Breaks do not stop the clock. |
| **30-minute break** | After 8 cumulative hours of driving without at least a 30-minute interruption, you must take a 30-minute break. |
| **60/70-hour limit** | You may not drive after 60 hours on duty in 7 days, or 70 hours in 8 days, depending on your carrier's schedule. |
| **34-hour restart** | 34 consecutive hours off duty resets your 60/70-hour cycle. |

## Sleeper berth splits

Drivers with a sleeper berth can split the 10-hour off-duty requirement, commonly into a 7/3 or 8/2 pattern, with one period of at least 7 or 8 hours in the sleeper and a second period of at least 2 or 3 hours off duty or in the berth. Done correctly, the paired periods can pause the 14-hour window. This is where many violations happen, so follow your ELD's guidance.

## Common exceptions

- **Adverse driving conditions** can extend the driving limit by up to two hours in specific cases.
- **Short-haul exception:** drivers who stay within 150 air miles of their reporting location and return within 14 hours may qualify for relief from some logging requirements.
- **Personal conveyance** and yard moves have their own rules.

The exceptions have detailed conditions. Do not rely on a summary for compliance decisions.

## How HOS affects your money

HOS caps the miles you can run. A truck averaging 55 mph over 11 driving hours covers a bit over 600 miles in a day; weekly, the 70-hour cycle is the real limit. That is why **deadhead and waiting time cost you legal hours**, not just fuel.
