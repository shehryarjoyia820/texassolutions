Insurance is often one of the largest fixed costs for an owner-operator, and one of the most confusing. With **trucking insurance explained** in plain English, you can see what each coverage does, which ones are required, and what to ask before you sign. This guide is general information, not insurance advice. Review your situation with a licensed insurance agent.

## Why insurance matters beyond the law

Without the right coverage you cannot get or keep active authority, most brokers will not set you up, and a single accident can wipe out years of savings. The goal is not to buy everything. It is to cover the risks that could put you out of business, at a price your rates can carry.

## Leased-on or your own authority?

Your needs depend on how you operate:

- **Your own authority (MC number):** you are the motor carrier. You need primary liability filed with FMCSA, plus the coverages your brokers and lender require.
- **Leased onto a carrier:** the carrier usually provides primary liability while you are under dispatch. You may still need non-trucking liability or bobtail, physical damage, and sometimes occupational accident coverage. Read your lease to see exactly what you must buy.

If you are still deciding, our guides on [getting your MC number](/blog/how-to-get-mc-number-authority-owner-operator.html) and [leasing onto an MC](/mc-lease.html) explain both paths.

## The main coverages

### Primary auto liability

Pays for injuries and property damage you cause to others with the truck. For carriers with their own authority, the insurer files proof of this coverage with FMCSA, and your authority cannot become or stay active without it.

FMCSA sets minimum amounts based on what you haul and the vehicle size. For many for-hire general freight carriers in heavier trucks, the minimum has been $750,000, with higher limits for certain hazardous materials. Many brokers and shippers require $1,000,000. Confirm current minimums for your operation with FMCSA.

### Motor truck cargo

Covers the freight you haul if it is damaged or lost. Most brokers require cargo coverage and will tell you the limit they need. Watch for:

- **Excluded commodities:** some policies exclude items such as electronics, alcohol, tobacco or certain high-value goods.
- **Reefer breakdown:** spoilage from a reefer unit failure may need a specific endorsement.
- **Unattended vehicle and theft conditions:** some policies limit theft coverage when the truck is left unattended.

### Physical damage

Covers your own truck and trailer for collision, and often for theft, fire and other losses (comprehensive). If the truck is financed or leased, the lender usually requires it. Choose a stated value that matches what it would cost to replace the truck, and a deductible you could pay tomorrow.

### Non-trucking liability and bobtail

For leased-on drivers. These cover liability when you are not under dispatch for the carrier. The wording varies between policies, so ask exactly when coverage applies and when it does not.

### General liability

Covers claims that are not caused by driving, such as damage at a customer's dock or injuries at your place of business. Some shippers and facilities require it.

### Occupational accident or workers' compensation

Covers you if you are hurt on the job. Owner-operators often buy occupational accident coverage, and some carriers require it in the lease. If you hire employees, workers' compensation rules depend on your state, so check with your agent and state agency.

### Trailer interchange and non-owned trailer

Covers damage to a trailer you do not own but are pulling under an agreement, such as in power-only or drop-and-hook work. Ask about it if you pull customer trailers. See [power-only dispatch](/power-only-dispatch.html) if that is your model.

## What affects your premium

Insurers look at many factors. Common ones include:

- Years of CDL experience and your driving record.
- How long your authority has been active.
- Your safety record and inspection history.
- What you haul and its value.
- Where the truck is garaged and how far you travel.
- The value and age of the truck and trailer.
- Coverage limits and deductibles you choose.
- Claims history.

You cannot change all of these, but a clean record, careful driving and fixing inspection violations quickly help over time.

## Worked example: choosing a deductible (hypothetical)

Your agent quotes physical damage two ways:

| Option | Deductible | Yearly premium |
|---|---|---|
| A | $1,000 | $4,800 |
| B | $2,500 | $4,200 |

Option B saves $600 a year but costs $1,500 more if you have a claim. If you go about two and a half years without a claim, B comes out ahead. If you cannot pay $2,500 in cash tomorrow, A may be safer. These numbers are only an example; real quotes vary widely.

## Questions to ask your insurance agent

1. Which coverages do I need for how I operate (own authority or leased on)?
2. What limits do my brokers, shippers and lender require?
3. What commodities are excluded from my cargo policy?
4. Does my cargo policy cover reefer breakdown, theft and unattended trucks?
5. Is my truck insured at a stated value that would replace it?
6. Who are the listed drivers, and what happens if someone else drives?
7. What radius or territory limits are in the policy?
8. How fast can you issue certificates of insurance to brokers?
9. How are payments structured, and what happens if one is late?
10. What do I do, step by step, right after an accident or cargo claim?

## Insurance checklist

- [ ] Coverage matches how you operate.
- [ ] Liability filing confirmed active with FMCSA.
- [ ] Limits meet broker and lender requirements.
- [ ] Cargo exclusions reviewed against what you haul.
- [ ] Physical damage value and deductible make sense.
- [ ] Certificates of insurance ready for broker packets.
- [ ] Premium included in your cost per mile.
- [ ] Renewal date on your calendar, with time to shop.
