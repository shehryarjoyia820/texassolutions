The **BOC-3 filing** is one of the first things FMCSA looks for when you apply for operating authority, and one of the easiest to overlook later. It is a short form, but without it your MC authority will not be granted, and problems with it can put existing authority at risk.

## What a BOC-3 is

BOC-3 stands for **Designation of Process Agents**. A process agent is a person or company in a given state that can receive court papers (service of process) on behalf of your business if someone files a legal action against you in that state.

Because trucks cross many states, FMCSA requires interstate carriers, brokers and freight forwarders to name an agent in each state where they operate. For carriers, that generally means every state you drive through or do business in, and in practice most carriers designate agents in all states plus the District of Columbia.

## Who needs a BOC-3

- **For-hire motor carriers** applying for interstate operating authority (an MC number).
- **Freight brokers.**
- **Freight forwarders.**

Owner-operators who lease onto another carrier and run under that carrier's authority generally do not need their own BOC-3. If you later get your own authority, you will. See [how to get your MC number](/blog/how-to-get-an-mc-number.html) for the whole sequence.

## Blanket agents vs. naming your own agents

### Blanket process agent company

Most carriers use a **blanket agent**: a company that maintains agents in every state and files one BOC-3 that covers them all. Benefits:

- One filing, all states.
- Filed electronically with FMCSA, so it usually shows up faster.
- The company keeps the agent list current.

When comparing blanket agent companies, ask:

- Is the fee one-time or annual? Are there renewal charges?
- Do they file electronically with FMCSA, and how fast?
- Will they send you a copy of the filing confirmation?
- How do they forward legal papers to you, and how fast?
- Do they offer anything you do not need, bundled into the price?

### Naming your own agents

You can name individuals or companies yourself and, under FMCSA rules, a carrier can file its own BOC-3 on paper. This takes more work, because you need a valid agent in every required state, and paper filings are slower. Few owner-operators go this route.

## Step-by-step: getting your BOC-3 filed

1. **Apply for your USDOT number and operating authority** through FMCSA's registration system.
2. **Choose a blanket process agent company** (or arrange your own agents).
3. **Give them your exact legal name, USDOT and MC or docket number, and address** as they appear on your FMCSA application.
4. **They file the BOC-3** with FMCSA electronically.
5. **Get a copy** of the filing for your records.
6. **Make sure insurance is also filed.** Authority is generally not granted until both the BOC-3 and required insurance filings are on record and the protest period has passed.
7. **Check FMCSA's public lookup** to confirm the BOC-3 shows as on file.

## When to update or replace your BOC-3

A BOC-3 does not usually expire, but you need a new filing when:

- **You change process agent companies.** The new company files a new BOC-3.
- **Your agent company goes out of business** or cancels your service.
- **Your legal business name changes,** such as moving from a sole proprietorship to an LLC with a new USDOT number. In that case, check whether you need new authority altogether.
- **FMCSA or a broker says it is missing.** Fix it right away.

## Worked example: a typical new-authority timeline

Every case is different, but here is a simplified sequence for a new carrier:

| Step | Who files | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Apply for USDOT and MC | You | Through FMCSA's online registration |
| BOC-3 | Blanket agent company | Usually filed within a short time after you order |
| Liability insurance filing | Your insurance company | Often the step that takes the longest |
| Protest period | FMCSA | A waiting period before authority becomes active |
| Authority granted | FMCSA | Check status online, then start broker setups |

If authority does not activate when you expect, check both the BOC-3 and insurance status first. A missing filing is the most common hold-up.

## Common BOC-3 problems

- **Name mismatch.** The name on the BOC-3 must match your FMCSA record.
- **Wrong docket number** entered by the agent company.
- **Assuming the insurance agent filed it.** Insurance agents file insurance, not BOC-3s, unless they say otherwise.
- **Paying for "annual BOC-3 renewal"** you may not need. Read the terms before you pay.
- **Ignoring legal papers** forwarded by your agent. If you receive service of process, talk to an attorney promptly.

The BOC-3 is one of several items that keep your authority in good standing, along with insurance, UCR and the MCS-150 update. Our guide on how to [keep your MC authority active](/blog/keep-your-mc-authority-active.html) puts all of them on one calendar.
