Choosing between a **box truck vs cargo van** is one of the first big decisions for a small freight business. Both can be run without a CDL in many cases, and both haul freight that semis do not want. But they differ a lot in cost, rules, capacity and the customers they attract.

## The short version

- **Cargo van:** lower cost, fewer rules, small loads, lower pay per load. Good for courier, expedite and testing the business.
- **Box truck:** higher cost, more compliance, pallets and liftgate freight, more customer types. Good for regional freight, final mile and retail or furniture work.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | Cargo van | Box truck (non-CDL) |
|---|---|---|
| Typical cargo | Boxes, parts, a few pallets | Multiple pallets, furniture, appliances |
| Liftgate | Rare | Common, often required |
| Driver license | Regular license | Regular license if under the CDL weight threshold |
| Federal rules | Fewer, since most vans are under main weight thresholds | More, often including DOT medical card, hours of service and inspections |
| Fuel use | Lower | Higher |
| Purchase price | Lower | Higher |
| Rate per mile | Lower | Higher |
| Customer types | Courier, expedite, small parcels | LTL-style loads, final mile, retail, partial loads |

Weight thresholds and exact rules change, so confirm current requirements with FMCSA and your state before buying either one.

## Startup cost and running cost

A cargo van is usually the cheapest way into for-hire freight. The purchase price is lower, insurance is often lower, and fuel and tires cost less. If money is tight, that matters, because a big payment in the slow first months is one of the most common reasons new carriers fail.

A box truck costs more in almost every line: purchase, insurance, fuel, tires, liftgate maintenance and possibly an ELD. In exchange, each load carries more freight and usually pays more.

### A simple hypothetical comparison

Say both vehicles run 8,000 miles a month.

- **Cargo van:** total monthly cost of $4,000, or $0.50 per mile. Average rate $1.20 per loaded mile with 25% empty miles. Revenue: 6,000 loaded miles x $1.20 = $7,200. Profit before your pay: **$3,200**.
- **Box truck:** total monthly cost of $7,200, or $0.90 per mile. Average rate $2.00 per loaded mile with 20% empty miles. Revenue: 6,400 loaded miles x $2.00 = $12,800. Profit before your pay: **$5,600**.

These numbers are made up for illustration. Your real rates and costs depend on your lanes, season and equipment. Plug in your own figures with the [cost per mile calculator](/cost-per-mile-calculator.html) and the [load profitability calculator](/load-profitability-calculator.html).

The key point: the box truck earns more in good months, but it also loses more in slow months. Its break-even is higher.

## Freight and customers

**Cargo vans** fit small, urgent freight: auto parts, medical samples, documents, small business deliveries and long-distance expedite runs where one small shipment needs to move right away.

**Box trucks** open more doors:

- Partial and LTL-style pallet loads from brokers
- Final mile and home delivery, often with a two-person crew
- Retail store deliveries and restocks
- Furniture, appliances and trade show freight
- Dedicated routes for local distributors

Many shippers require a liftgate because their receivers do not have a dock. A box truck with a working liftgate can take those loads; a van usually cannot.

## Rules and compliance

This is where the two differ the most. Many federal motor carrier rules apply once a vehicle crosses certain weight thresholds in interstate commerce. Most cargo vans stay under them. Most box trucks are over at least the first one.

For a box truck in interstate commerce, expect to deal with:

- USDOT number and, for for-hire freight, FMCSA operating authority
- DOT medical card for the driver
- Hours-of-service rules and, in many cases, an ELD
- Annual vehicle inspection and driver qualification records
- Drug and alcohol testing only if the vehicle requires a CDL

A for-hire van hauling across state lines generally still needs operating authority. Confirm all of this with FMCSA, since thresholds and exceptions are specific. Our guide on [how to get an MC number](/blog/how-to-get-mc-number-authority-owner-operator.html) covers the authority side.

## Which should you choose?

Start with a **cargo van** if:

- Your budget is limited and you want low fixed costs
- You have local courier or expedite customers lined up
- You want to learn the business before taking on more risk

Start with a **box truck** if:

- You have access to pallet, liftgate or final mile freight
- You can handle the extra compliance work
- You have enough cash reserve to survive a slow month or two

Either way, decide based on freight you can actually get. A vehicle without customers is just a payment.

## Moving from a van to a box truck

If you already run a van, these are signs it may be time to step up:

1. You turn down loads every week because they will not fit.
2. Customers keep asking about liftgate service.
3. Your van runs full most days and you have steady demand.
4. You have savings to cover the larger payment and a few slow weeks.
