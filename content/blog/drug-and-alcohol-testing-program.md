A **DOT drug and alcohol testing program** is one of the first things a new carrier needs and one of the most common reasons new carriers fail their safety audit. If you or any of your drivers need a CDL for the vehicle you operate, the program is not optional. Here is how it works and how to set it up as a one-truck or small fleet operation.

## Who must have a testing program

The FMCSA testing rules (49 CFR Part 382, with procedures in Part 40) apply to employers of drivers who operate commercial motor vehicles that require a CDL. That includes owner-operators who are both the employer and the driver. Typical semi trucks are covered.

Non-CDL operations, such as many cargo van, box truck and hotshot setups under the CDL weight thresholds, are generally outside Part 382. Some brokers, shippers or insurers may still ask for testing as a business requirement. When in doubt, check with FMCSA about your exact equipment.

## Setup checklist for a new carrier

Do these in order, before your first load:

1. [ ] **Choose a consortium or third-party administrator (C/TPA).** It manages the random pool, schedules tests through collection sites, and keeps many of the records. Ask what is included: random selection, collection site network, medical review officer, annual summary, Clearinghouse support and post-accident help after hours.
2. [ ] **Adopt a written testing policy** that covers the required topics, and give a copy to every driver. Keep signed receipts.
3. [ ] **Name a designated employer representative (DER)**, the person who receives results and removes drivers from duty. For a one-truck carrier, that is usually you.
4. [ ] **Register in the Clearinghouse** as an employer and, if you are an owner-operator, designate your C/TPA so it can report on your behalf.
5. [ ] **Get a pre-employment drug test** with a verified negative result before any safety-sensitive work.
6. [ ] **Run a pre-employment full Clearinghouse query** on each CDL driver, with the driver's electronic consent in the Clearinghouse.
7. [ ] **Enroll every CDL driver in the random pool**, including yourself.
8. [ ] **Train supervisors** on reasonable suspicion if you have anyone supervising drivers. The rules require training on both drug and alcohol signs.
9. [ ] **Set up a confidential file** for testing records, separate from the DQ file.

## The six types of tests

| Test | When it happens | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-employment | Before first safety-sensitive work | Drug test required; negative result must be received first |
| Random | Unannounced, throughout the year | FMCSA sets minimum annual rates for drugs and alcohol |
| Post-accident | After qualifying crashes | Time limits apply for alcohol and drug tests |
| Reasonable suspicion | When a trained supervisor observes specific signs | Must be documented |
| Return-to-duty | After a violation, before returning | Directly observed collection for drugs |
| Follow-up | After return to duty | Unannounced, set by the substance abuse professional |

FMCSA publishes the minimum random testing rates each year. In recent years they have been 50 percent of the average number of driver positions for drugs and 10 percent for alcohol, but confirm the current rates on the FMCSA website, since they can change.

### Post-accident testing basics

Post-accident testing is where small carriers most often get confused. In general, testing is required after a crash involving a fatality, and after a crash where the CMV driver gets a citation and someone is injured and treated away from the scene, or a vehicle is towed away with disabling damage. Alcohol tests have short time limits (the rule targets within 2 hours and sets an outer limit of 8 hours), and drug tests have a longer outer limit (32 hours). Keep your C/TPA's after-hours number in the truck and confirm the current criteria in Part 382.

## How the Clearinghouse fits in

The FMCSA Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse is an online database of CDL driver violations: positive tests, refusals and return-to-duty progress. It works in a few directions:

- **Employers query it** before hiring a CDL driver and at least once a year for current drivers.
- **Employers and C/TPAs report** violations and certain other information.
- **Drivers register** so they can give consent and view their own records.

State licensing agencies now use Clearinghouse information too. Under rules that took effect in late 2024, a driver in "prohibited" status can lose the commercial driving privilege on their license until they complete return to duty. Confirm current details with FMCSA.

Clearinghouse queries are purchased in plans through the Clearinghouse itself. Budget for them as a small, recurring cost.

## What happens with a violation

If a driver tests positive, refuses a test or otherwise violates the rules:

1. **Remove the driver from safety-sensitive work immediately.**
2. **Report the violation** to the Clearinghouse within the required time (your C/TPA or MRO may handle part of this).
3. **Give the driver a list of substance abuse professionals (SAPs).**
4. The driver completes the SAP's evaluation and recommended education or treatment.
5. The driver passes a **return-to-duty test**.
6. The driver is placed on a **follow-up testing plan** set by the SAP.

The carrier does not have to rehire a driver after a violation. But no carrier can let that driver operate a CDL vehicle until return to duty is complete.

## Records to keep

Retention periods differ by record. In general, positive results, refusals and SAP records are kept longer (several years), while negative results are kept for a shorter period. Your C/TPA may hold many of these, but you are still responsible for being able to produce them. Ask your provider exactly which records they keep and for how long, and check Part 382 Subpart D for the current schedule.

A missing testing program is on FMCSA's list of automatic failure violations in the [new entrant safety audit](/blog/new-entrant-safety-audit-guide.html).
