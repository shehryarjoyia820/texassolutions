You have the truck, the insurance and an active MC. Now the hard part: **how to get loads with a new MC number**. Many brokers will not book a carrier until the authority is a few months old, and every broker has its own setup process. This guide is the step-by-step plan we use for **loads for new authority carriers**, whether you run a semi, a hotshot or a box truck.

## Step 1: build one complete broker setup packet

Do this once, before you need a load. Keep it as a single PDF and as separate files.

- MC authority letter (or a screenshot of active authority on FMCSA).
- W-9 in the exact legal name on your authority.
- Certificate of insurance from your agent, with auto liability and cargo limits listed. Ask your agent how fast they can issue certificates naming each broker.
- Notice of assignment and letter of release, if you use a factoring company.
- Bank details for payment, if you do not factor.
- A short carrier profile: equipment, trailer length, lanes, contact phone and email.

A packet that is complete the first time is the quickest way through broker onboarding. Missing documents are the most common reason a new carrier waits days for setup. That is why **broker setup packet help** is the first thing we do for a new client.

## Step 2: find brokers that accept new authority

Brokers fall into three groups:

1. **Will not book new MCs** until the authority reaches a set age.
2. **Will book new MCs** if the packet is complete and insurance is verified.
3. **Will book new MCs on certain freight** (often less time-sensitive or lower value loads) while you build history.

Your job is to find groups 2 and 3 and get set up with them before you need a load. Ask directly when you call: "Do you work with new authority?" You save more time asking that up front than you spend on setups that go nowhere.

## Step 3: use load boards the right way

Load boards are where most new carriers find their first loads. Filter for brokers that show they accept new authority, and call fast: good loads go in minutes. Read our [load board guide](/blog/truck-loads-and-load-boards-guide.html) and [best load boards for owner-operators](/blog/best-load-boards-for-owner-operators.html).

## Step 4: pick first lanes that set up the next load

Do not chase the single highest rate. Pick lanes that deliver into markets with plenty of outbound freight, so the reload is easy. Use the [deadhead miles calculator](/deadhead-miles-calculator.html) to see what an empty leg really costs.

## Step 5: negotiate, even with a new MC

A new MC does not have to mean the lowest rate. Know your [cost per mile](/cost-per-mile-calculator.html), ask for more on short-notice loads, and walk away from freight that does not cover your costs. See [how to negotiate freight rates](/blog/how-to-negotiate-freight-rates.html).

## Step 6: deliver perfectly and get rebooked

For the first months, every load is an audition. Be on time, answer check calls, send clear photos of the bill of lading and paperwork the same day. Brokers rebook carriers who make their job easy, and repeat freight is how a new authority stops depending on load boards.

## Common mistakes new authority carriers make

- Waiting until the authority is active to start broker setups.
- Taking every cheap load to "build history" and running at a loss.
- Hauling under someone else's MC: it is risky and does not build your own record.
- Paying a large upfront fee to anyone who promises guaranteed loads. Read [how to avoid truck dispatch scams](/blog/how-to-choose-a-truck-dispatcher-avoid-scams.html).
