**UCR registration** is a once-a-year task that is easy to forget until a roadside officer or a broker asks for it. This guide explains who must register for the Unified Carrier Registration program, how the fee is figured, and how to file without overpaying.

## What UCR is

The Unified Carrier Registration program is a federal law program run by a board of state and federal representatives, with fees set through FMCSA rulemaking. Fees collected go to participating states, largely to support motor carrier safety programs and enforcement.

UCR does not replace your USDOT number, operating authority, IRP or IFTA. It is a separate annual registration. Brokers and shippers may check it during carrier setup, and roadside officers in participating states can see whether you are registered for the current year.

## Who needs UCR

UCR generally applies to businesses that operate in interstate or international commerce, including:

- **For-hire motor carriers** with interstate operations,
- **Private motor carriers** of property in interstate commerce,
- **Exempt carriers** that haul exempt commodities interstate,
- **Freight brokers, freight forwarders and leasing companies.**

Interstate commerce can include a trip that stays inside one state if the freight is part of a longer interstate move, such as a port or rail container. If you are not sure, check with the UCR program before assuming you are exempt.

If you lease onto another carrier and run under its authority, the carrier usually registers and counts your truck. If you have your own authority, it is on you.

## How UCR fees work

UCR fees are set in **brackets based on the number of commercial motor vehicles** you operate. Brokers and freight forwarders that do not operate trucks pay the lowest bracket. The fee amounts are adjusted from time to time by FMCSA, so look up the current table on the official UCR website rather than relying on an old invoice.

### Counting your vehicles

The fleet count generally includes power units (trucks and tractors) you operate in interstate commerce. The program gives carriers a choice of counting method, typically based on your vehicle count on your most recent MCS-150 or on the vehicles you operated over a recent 12-month period. Trailers are generally not counted. Check current UCR guidance for the exact rules before you file.

### Worked example: choosing the right bracket

Here is a hypothetical example. Bracket boundaries and fees below are placeholders, not actual UCR figures.

- Your MCS-150 shows **3 power units**.
- You sold one truck mid-year and now run **2**.
- The current UCR table puts 0-2 vehicles in the lowest bracket and 3-5 in the next.
- Depending on the counting method you are allowed to use, you might fall in either bracket. Keep records (titles, bills of sale, MCS-150 update) that support the count you report.

The takeaway: keep your MCS-150 updated, because it is one source the UCR program uses for your fleet size. See how to [keep your MC authority active](/blog/keep-your-mc-authority-active.html) for MCS-150 timing.

## When to register

UCR is registered by **calendar year**. Registration for the next year typically opens in the fall, and states generally begin enforcing the new year's registration early in the new year. Exact opening and enforcement dates are announced by the UCR program each year, so check the official site and register early.

If you get new authority partway through the year, register for the current year before you start hauling interstate. If you plan to stop running interstate or close the business, check with the UCR program before skipping a year, and update your FMCSA records so your status is accurate.

## Base state and participating states

Your **base state** is generally the state where your principal place of business is located. Most states participate in UCR. If your home state does not participate, you register through a nearby participating state as directed by the program.

## Step-by-step: how to register

1. **Have your USDOT number** and business information ready.
2. **Go to the official UCR registration website.** Be careful with search results; look-alike sites charge extra service fees.
3. **Confirm your company details** pulled from FMCSA records.
4. **Choose your classification** (carrier, broker, freight forwarder or leasing company, or a combination).
5. **Enter your vehicle count** using an allowed counting method.
6. **Pay the fee** for your bracket.
7. **Save your receipt** and keep a copy in the cab and in your office files.

### UCR checklist

- [ ] USDOT number active and MCS-150 up to date
- [ ] Classification matches what you actually do
- [ ] Vehicle count supported by records
- [ ] Registered for the correct calendar year
- [ ] Paid on the official site, not a third-party look-alike
- [ ] Receipt saved digitally and on paper
- [ ] Calendar reminder set for next fall
