Every ELD on the market claims to be simple and compliant, so **how to choose an ELD** comes down to asking the right questions. The right device keeps you legal at inspections, does not fight you every morning and does not lock you into a contract you regret.

This guide compares ELDs by features and questions instead of brands. Rules mentioned here are summarized from the federal ELD rule as we understand it; confirm current requirements with FMCSA.

## Step 1: Make sure you actually need one

The federal ELD rule applies to most drivers who must keep records of duty status. Common exceptions include:

- **Older engines.** Vehicles with an engine model year older than 2000.
- **Short-haul drivers** who qualify for the short-haul exception and use time records instead of logs.
- **Drivers who keep logs 8 days or fewer** in any 30-day period.
- **Driveaway-towaway operations**, in certain situations.

If you fit an exception, you may still choose an ELD for tracking and IFTA mileage, but you are not required to use one. Confirm with FMCSA before you skip it.

## Step 2: Check compliance first

An ELD that is not properly registered is a violation waiting to happen.

- **Registered list.** ELDs are self-certified by the manufacturer and listed on FMCSA's registered ELD list. Confirm the exact model and version you are buying is on it.
- **Revoked list.** FMCSA also publishes revoked devices. If yours is revoked, you have a limited time to replace it. Check both lists before you buy and every few months after.
- **Data transfer at inspections.** The rule allows telematics transfer (web services and email) or local transfer (USB and Bluetooth). Ask which method the device supports and practice it before an inspector asks you to.
- **Malfunction support.** The provider should give you the instruction sheets you must carry. See our [ELD malfunction rules](/blog/eld-malfunction-rules.html) guide for what that packet includes.

## Step 3: Compare the features that matter day to day

Most ELDs record the same required data. The differences show up in daily use.

| Feature | What to look for | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Driver app | Clear clocks for 11, 14, 30-minute break and 60/70 cycle | You check it dozens of times a day |
| Split sleeper support | Calculates 7/3 and 8/2 pairings automatically | Avoids manual math mistakes |
| PC and yard moves | Can be enabled per driver, with annotations | Needed for honest off-duty and yard movement |
| Unidentified driving | Easy to accept, assign or annotate | Unresolved records cause audit headaches |
| Edits | Clear workflow for proposed edits and driver approval | Keeps your logs accurate and defensible |
| DVIR | Electronic inspection reports | Saves paper and creates a record |
| IFTA mileage | Miles by state, exportable | Speeds up quarterly fuel tax filing |
| GPS tracking | Location and history | Helpful for dispatch and detention proof |
| Document scanning | Upload BOLs and receipts | Faster billing and factoring |
| Offline mode | Records properly without cell signal | Rural lanes and dead zones |

Do not pay for fleet features you will never use. A one-truck owner-operator needs a reliable driver app, accurate clocks and easy IFTA reports more than a complex fleet dashboard.

## Step 4: Check hardware fit

- **Connector type.** Heavy trucks commonly use 9-pin diagnostic ports, while some newer trucks use different connectors. Ask which cables are included and what an adapter costs.
- **Mounting.** A portable ELD (phone or tablet) generally must be mounted in a fixed position while the truck is moving and be visible from the normal driving position. A solid mount matters.
- **Phone or tablet.** Bring your own device or use a provided tablet? Dedicated devices avoid conflicts with personal apps and notifications.
- **Installation.** Plug-and-play units are easy to move between trucks. Hardwired units may be more stable but harder to swap.

## Step 5: Understand the real cost

ELD pricing comes in many shapes. Compare total cost over the contract, not just the monthly number.

**Hypothetical example:** Option A has no hardware cost and a higher monthly fee on a 36-month contract. Option B charges for hardware up front with a lower monthly fee and no contract.

- Option A: 36 months x $40 = $1,440
- Option B: $250 hardware + (36 months x $30) = $1,330

Option B is cheaper over three years and can be canceled any time, but costs more on day one. These numbers are made up for illustration; get written quotes and run your own comparison. Add the result to your fixed costs in the [cost per mile calculator](/cost-per-mile-calculator.html).

Watch for these extras:

- Activation or setup fees
- Early termination fees
- Charges for extra cables, mounts or replacement hardware
- Paid add-ons for IFTA reports, tracking history or document scanning
- Price increases after a promotional period

## Questions to ask before you sign

Use this checklist when you talk to any ELD provider:

- [ ] Is this exact model on FMCSA's registered ELD list today?
- [ ] Which inspection transfer methods does it support?
- [ ] Is support available 24/7, including nights and weekends, by phone?
- [ ] What happens if the device fails on the road? How fast is replacement hardware shipped?
- [ ] How does the app handle split sleeper, PC and yard moves?
- [ ] Can I export my logs and IFTA data if I leave?
- [ ] What is the contract length, and what does it cost to cancel early?
- [ ] Which phones or tablets are supported?
- [ ] Does it work without cell signal, and how does it sync later?
- [ ] Can it share location with a dispatcher or customer, and can I control that?

Read online reviews from other small carriers, but focus on patterns about support and reliability, not single complaints.
