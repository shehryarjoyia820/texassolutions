Freight does not move evenly through the year. Harvests, retail stocking, holidays and weather all shift how many loads are posted and how many trucks are chasing them. A simple **freight market calendar** helps you plan cash flow, maintenance and home time around the months that tend to pay best and the months that tend to drag.

## Why seasonality matters for an owner-operator

Spot rates move with the balance between loads and trucks. When shippers have more freight than the market has trucks, rates rise. When trucks outnumber loads, rates fall and brokers have more leverage.

For a one-truck operation, those swings show up directly in weekly gross. Knowing roughly when they happen lets you:

- Build a cash reserve during strong months to cover the slow ones.
- Schedule maintenance and home time when the truck would earn the least.
- Position the truck near busy regions before demand peaks.
- Negotiate harder when capacity is tight, and accept that some weeks are about covering costs.

Keep in mind that the wider economy, fuel prices and regulations can override normal seasonal patterns. Treat this calendar as a starting point, then watch what your own lanes do.

## Month-by-month freight calendar

| Period | What usually happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| January | Post-holiday slowdown, returns freight, winter weather | Expect softer rates; plan maintenance and paperwork |
| February | Still soft in many lanes; early produce from southern regions | Watch southern reefer freight; protect cash |
| March | Freight builds; quarter-end push; spring produce grows | Look for better outbound options; firm up rates |
| April - May | Produce moves north; construction picks up; inspection blitzes | Strong month for reefer and flatbed in many areas |
| June | Quarter-end push; hurricane season begins | Watch for storm-related disruptions and relief freight |
| July | Holiday week squeeze around July 4; summer produce | Plan around the holiday; check reefer demand |
| August - October | Retail stocking for back-to-school and the holidays; harvest freight | Often the strongest stretch for dry van and reefer |
| November | Thanksgiving squeeze; holiday food and retail freight | Book ahead; expect tight capacity around the holiday |
| December | Early month busy, then slows near Christmas | Decide on home time; book loads before the holiday lull |

This table is a general pattern, not a forecast. Your region, trailer type and customer base can shift every line of it.

### Spring: produce and construction

Produce season is one of the biggest seasonal drivers for refrigerated freight. Harvests usually start in the southern states and move north as the weather warms, which pulls reefer capacity toward those growing regions. If you run a reefer, the [reefer loads guide](/blog/reefer-loads-refrigerated-dispatch-guide.html) covers how to work those markets.

Spring also brings construction back. Flatbed demand for lumber, steel and building materials usually firms up as job sites reopen after winter.

**Inspection blitzes.**

CVSA runs enforcement events during the year, including a multi-day roadside inspection blitz that is usually held in spring. Some drivers park during those days, which can tighten capacity in certain areas. Check the CVSA website for the dates each year, and make sure your truck and paperwork are ready regardless.

### Summer: holidays, heat and storms

Holiday weeks such as Memorial Day, July 4 and Labor Day usually cause short capacity squeezes. Many drivers take time off, while shippers still need loads moved before and after the holiday.

Hurricane season in the Atlantic runs roughly from June through November. Storms can shut down lanes, delay pickups and create sudden demand for relief and rebuilding freight. Keep an eye on the weather when you book loads into the Gulf Coast or the Southeast.

Summer heat also matters for reefer operators, since temperature-sensitive loads need reliable equipment and careful pre-cooling.

### Fall: peak season

For many dry van and reefer carriers, late summer and fall are the strongest stretch of the year. Retailers stock up for back-to-school and holiday sales, and harvest freight adds to the volume. Read our [peak season guide](/blog/peak-season-trucking-loads-holiday-freight.html) for how to make the most of it.

Peak season is also when rate confirmations get rushed. Read every rate con carefully, confirm detention and layover terms, and do not let a busy week push you into a load that does not cover your costs.

### Winter: the slow stretch

After the holidays, retail freight drops and many lanes go soft. January and February are often the hardest months for owner-operators who rely only on the spot market. Winter weather also adds risk, delays and chain-law requirements in mountain states.

Use this time to:

1. Do major maintenance and inspections.
2. Review last year's numbers and your cost per mile.
3. Update your broker packet and insurance certificates.
4. Build relationships with shippers or brokers that move steady freight year-round.

## Weekly and monthly patterns too

Seasonality is not just about months. Many lanes also have weekly rhythms:

- **Monday and Friday** can be busier for posted loads in some markets.
- **Friday afternoon** loads may pay more if a broker needs a truck before the weekend.
- **Month-end and quarter-end** often bring a push as shippers try to move product before the books close.

Track these patterns in your own lanes for a few months. A simple spreadsheet with date, lane, rate and load count will show you more than any general rule.

## Worked example: planning a slow month

Here is a hypothetical example. Say your all-in cost is $1.90 per mile and you usually run 10,000 miles a month. That is $19,000 a month in costs.

- In a strong month, you average $2.60 per mile: $26,000 gross, about $7,000 above cost.
- In a slow month, you average $2.05 per mile: $20,500 gross, only $1,500 above cost.

If you set aside part of the strong-month profit, the slow month stops being a crisis. Check your own numbers with the [cost per mile calculator](/cost-per-mile-calculator.html).
