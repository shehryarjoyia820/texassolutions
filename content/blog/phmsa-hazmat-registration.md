**PHMSA hazmat registration** is a yearly filing with the Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration, part of the US Department of Transportation. If your truck hauls placarded hazardous materials, you almost certainly need it, and brokers and shippers will often ask for your certificate before tendering hazmat freight.

## What PHMSA registration is (and is not)

The registration program lives in 49 CFR Part 107, Subpart G. It applies to people and companies who **offer for transport or transport** certain hazardous materials in commerce. The fees fund emergency response planning and training grants.

It is easy to confuse with other hazmat credentials:

| Credential | Who holds it | Issued by |
|---|---|---|
| Hazmat endorsement (H) | The driver | State licensing agency, after a TSA threat assessment |
| PHMSA registration | The company (carrier or shipper) | PHMSA |
| Hazmat safety permit | The carrier, for certain high-hazard materials | FMCSA |
| USDOT number with hazmat marked | The carrier | FMCSA, via the MCS-150 |

You can need all four. Our [hazmat endorsement guide](/blog/hazmat-endorsement-guide.html) covers the driver side.

## Which loads trigger registration

As we understand the current rule, you must register if you transport or offer any of the following during the registration year:

- Any quantity of hazardous material that **requires placards**
- A highway route-controlled quantity of a Class 7 (radioactive) material
- More than a set weight of Division 1.1, 1.2 or 1.3 explosives in a motor vehicle
- More than a set volume per package of a material that is extremely toxic by inhalation (hazard zone A)
- Hazmat in a bulk packaging above a set capacity (large tanks and bulk bins)
- A non-bulk shipment of a single placarded class above a set gross weight

For most owner-operators the first line settles it: **if the load is placarded, plan on registering.** The exact weights and capacities for the other items are in the regulation; check them on the PHMSA website if you haul bulk or specialty hazmat.

There are a few exceptions, for example for certain agricultural operations and government agencies. Do not assume one applies to you without reading it.

## The registration year and fees

- The registration year runs **July 1 through June 30**.
- You can register for one, two or three years at once.
- The fee depends on whether you qualify as a small business under the Small Business Administration size standard for your industry, plus a processing fee. Not-for-profit organizations have their own rate.
- Fees have changed over time, so check the current amount on PHMSA's site before you file.

Because the year does not line up with the calendar year, it is easy to forget. Put a reminder in your compliance calendar for early June. Our guide on [keeping your MC authority active](/blog/keep-your-mc-authority-active.html) has a full list of yearly deadlines worth tracking alongside it, including [UCR registration](/blog/ucr-registration-guide.html).

## How to file, step by step

1. **Confirm you need it.** List the hazmat you expect to haul this year and check each against the thresholds above.
2. **Gather your information.** Legal business name, address, USDOT number, the hazard classes you will transport, and your business size information.
3. **Decide small business or not.** Use the SBA size standard for your industry code. Most single-truck owner-operators qualify as small businesses, but confirm it.
4. **File online with PHMSA.** Use the hazmat registration system on PHMSA's official website and pay by card or another accepted method. Avoid third-party sites that charge extra to "process" the filing unless you deliberately want that service.
5. **Download and save your certificate.** You get a registration number and a certificate of registration.
6. **Put proof in every truck.** A copy of the certificate, or another document showing the registration number, must be on each vehicle hauling regulated hazmat, and you must be able to show it to enforcement on request.
7. **Keep your records.** Keep a copy at your principal place of business for the period the rule requires.

## What to keep in the truck

When you run placarded freight, a simple hazmat folder in the cab saves a lot of trouble at inspections:

- PHMSA registration certificate (current year)
- Shipping papers, within reach and on top or tabbed
- Emergency response information for each material
- Your hazmat training certificate or record
- Hazmat safety permit, if the material requires one
- Placard set for the classes you haul

Officers check hazmat loads more closely. Read our guide on [DOT roadside inspections](/blog/dot-roadside-inspections.html) to know what they look at.

## Common mistakes

- **Letting it lapse on July 1.** A load you haul on July 2 under last year's certificate is not covered.
- **Registering under the wrong name.** Use the same legal name as your USDOT and MC records.
- **Thinking the shipper's registration covers you.** Shippers and carriers each register for their own activity.
- **Paying the large-business fee by mistake, or the small-business fee when you do not qualify.** Check the size standard each time you renew, especially as you add trucks.
- **Not updating the MCS-150.** If you start hauling hazmat, your USDOT record should reflect it.

## A simple example

Hypothetical: a one-truck carrier hauls dry van freight and starts accepting the occasional load of placarded paint. That first placarded load makes the carrier a hazmat transporter for that registration year. Before booking it, the owner checks insurance with the agent, files PHMSA registration as a small business, updates the MCS-150, completes hazmat employee training, and puts the certificate in the truck. The paperwork takes a few hours spread over a week, which is why it is better done before a broker offers the load, not after.

Thresholds, fees and filing methods can change. Always confirm current requirements directly with PHMSA and FMCSA, and talk to your insurance agent before hauling a new hazard class.
