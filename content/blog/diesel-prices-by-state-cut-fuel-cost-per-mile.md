Fuel is one of the biggest bills an owner-operator pays, and it changes every day. Two numbers decide what it costs you: **the price per gallon** and **your miles per gallon**. You control more of both than you might think.

## Your fuel cost per mile

The formula is simple:

**Fuel cost per mile = diesel price per gallon / miles per gallon**

A loaded semi averaging about 6.3 mpg (the FHWA national average for combination trucks) with diesel at $6.00 a gallon spends about **$0.95 per mile** on fuel. On a 2,500-mile week that is close to $2,400.

Smaller trucks do better per mile but carry less. A hotshot averaging around 9-10 mpg loaded, or a 26 ft box truck at 8.5-11 mpg, spends roughly half as much per mile as a semi.

Try your own truck, weight and state in the [truck fuel cost calculator](/truck-fuel-cost-calculator.html).

## Why diesel prices differ by state

Diesel can differ by **more than $2 a gallon** between the cheapest and most expensive states. The main reasons:

- **State fuel taxes.** These vary widely and are built into the pump price.
- **Distance from refineries and pipelines.** Gulf Coast states such as Texas, Louisiana and Mississippi are close to refining capacity.
- **Fuel specifications.** California requires its own cleaner blend and has additional fees, which is why it is usually the most expensive.
- **Local competition** at truck stops along major corridors.

## Seven ways to cut fuel cost per mile

1. **Plan fuel stops by state.** Filling 150 gallons where diesel is $1 cheaper saves $150 on one stop.
2. **Cut deadhead miles.** Empty miles burn fuel with no revenue. Planning the next load before this one delivers is the single biggest lever.
3. **Slow down.** Above about 62 mph, a semi loses roughly 0.1 mpg for every extra mph.
4. **Idle less.** A semi burns about 0.8 gallons per hour at idle.
5. **Keep tires properly inflated** and check alignment.
6. **Know your load weight.** NACFE research puts the fuel penalty at about 0.5-0.6% for every extra 1,000 lbs.
7. **Book loads that cover fuel.** Know your fuel cost per mile before accepting a rate.
