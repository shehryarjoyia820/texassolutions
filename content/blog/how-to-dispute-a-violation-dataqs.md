If a roadside inspection report or crash record on your USDOT number is wrong, you do not have to live with it. FMCSA's **DataQs** system lets carriers and drivers ask for a review of inaccurate safety data, and a successful request can remove or change a violation that is hurting your CSA percentiles.

## What DataQs is (and is not)

DataQs is FMCSA's online system for submitting a **Request for Data Review (RDR)**. FMCSA routes each request to the agency that owns the data, which for roadside inspections is usually the state enforcement agency whose officer did the inspection.

DataQs is for data that is **wrong or incomplete**. It is not a way to argue that a correct violation was unfair or to remove a violation because you later fixed the problem. Requests that only express disagreement tend to be closed quickly.

## Situations where a DataQs request makes sense

- **Wrong carrier.** The inspection was assigned to your USDOT number but the truck was running under another carrier's authority, or the number was entered wrong.
- **Wrong driver or vehicle.** A different driver's name, license or VIN appears on the report.
- **Duplicate record.** The same inspection or crash shows up twice.
- **Factually wrong violation.** For example, an hours-of-service violation your ELD records clearly disprove, or a defect code that does not match what was found.
- **Citation dismissed or found not guilty.** If a violation also led to a citation that was later dismissed or ended in a not-guilty result in court, FMCSA policy generally allows the violation to be updated in its data. A conviction of a different or lesser charge may also change how it is recorded.
- **Crash preventability.** For certain types of crashes, FMCSA's Crash Preventability Determination Program lets you submit a request through DataQs to have a crash reviewed as not preventable. Eligible crash types are defined by FMCSA, so check the current list.

## Evidence that tends to help

A request is only as strong as the proof attached. Gather what applies:

- The **inspection report** itself, with the report number.
- **ELD records** and supporting documents (fuel receipts, bills of lading, toll records) for hours-of-service disputes.
- **Lease agreement** and the operating carrier's information for wrong-carrier disputes.
- **Photos** taken at the scene with date and time.
- **Court documents** showing dismissal or not-guilty results for citations.
- **Police reports** and other records for crash reviews.
- A **repair invoice dated before** the inspection, if it proves a part was compliant at the time.

Label files clearly and keep them readable. A reviewer should understand your point without hunting through pages.

## Step-by-step: filing a DataQs request

1. **Find the record.** Pull the inspection or crash from your FMCSA portal or SMS results, and note the report number, date and state.
2. **Create or log in to your DataQs account.** Use the account type that matches you: motor carrier, driver, or other.
3. **Start a new request** and choose the correct category, such as inspection or crash, and the specific issue type.
4. **Identify the record** by report number and state.
5. **Write a short, factual explanation.** Say what is wrong, what is correct, and what evidence shows it. Keep the tone professional.
6. **Attach your evidence.**
7. **Submit and save your confirmation.**
8. **Watch for updates.** Reviewers may ask questions. Respond quickly, inside DataQs, so the request does not stall or close.
9. **Check your data** once a decision is made. Changes can take some time to flow into your SMS results.

### A sample explanation

Here is the kind of short, specific statement that works well. Adjust it to your facts:

> Inspection report [number], [state], [date]. Violation 395.8(e) (false log) was recorded. The driver's ELD records and the attached fuel receipts, timestamped in [city] at [time], show the logged location and duty status were accurate. We request the violation be reviewed and removed. ELD data file and receipts attached.

Avoid long stories, blame, or comments about the officer. Stick to the record and the evidence.

## If your request is denied

Read the reason. Sometimes a request fails only because evidence was missing or unclear. In that case you may be able to submit a **request for reconsideration** with better documentation, depending on the state's process. If the record is accurate, the best path is to let time and clean inspections reduce its effect; see [CSA scores explained](/blog/csa-scores-explained.html) for how violations age.

If a citation is involved and you plan to fight it in court, talk to an attorney who handles traffic and trucking cases. The court outcome can matter for what you can later request in DataQs.

## Mistakes that sink DataQs requests

- Filing without any evidence.
- Arguing a violation is minor instead of showing it is inaccurate.
- Filing the same request many times.
- Ignoring reviewer questions until the request closes.
- Waiting too long. Older records are harder to document, and the violation is affecting your scores the whole time.

## Prevent the next one

The cheapest violation is the one that never gets written. Keep your truck and paperwork ready for [DOT roadside inspections](/blog/dot-roadside-inspections.html), run legal hours, and review every inspection report before leaving the site. Know which defects put you [out of service](/blog/out-of-service-criteria.html), because those carry the most weight. If you share a truck under someone else's authority, read our guide to [MC lease options](/mc-lease.html) so records land on the right USDOT number.

This guide is general information. Procedures, eligible request types and state processes can change, so check the current guidance in DataQs and on FMCSA's website.
