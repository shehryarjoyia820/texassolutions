For most small carriers, the search for truck loads starts on a load board. It is often the first place a new authority finds freight, and it remains a daily tool for many experienced owner-operators. This guide explains what a **load board** is, how carriers use one, and how to judge the loads for trucks you see posted.

If you are working out how to find loads for trucks you own or drive, the load board is where most carriers start. You will also hear them called truck boards, a loading board or a loads board; a load board for truckers is the same tool whatever the name, and most load boards for trucking list truckload, partial and expedited freight side by side. Some carriers keep one truckload load board for long hauls and a second, smaller board for local freight; load boards for truckers differ mainly in which brokers post on them.

## What a load board is

A load board is an online marketplace. Freight brokers, and sometimes shippers, post loads that need a truck. Carriers and dispatchers search those posts and contact the poster to book.

A typical load post shows:

- Origin and destination
- Pickup date and sometimes a delivery window
- Equipment type (van, reefer, flatbed, step deck, power only, hotshot and so on)
- Weight and length
- Posted rate, or no rate at all
- The broker's company name, contact and MC number
- Sometimes the broker's credit score and average days to pay

Carriers can also **post their truck**: where it will be empty and when. Brokers search those posts and call carriers with matching loads.

## Common load boards

These are some of the well-known names. We describe them only in general terms, because features and prices change. Check each provider's website for current details.

- **DAT load board.** One of the largest load board networks in the US, with load search, truck posting, market rate data and broker credit information depending on the plan.
- **Truckstop load board.** Another large, long-running network, with load search, rate tools and broker payment and credit information on its plans.
- **123Loadboard.** A load board often used by smaller carriers, with load search and related tools.
- **Amazon Relay.** Not a traditional broker board. It is Amazon's own platform for booking Amazon freight with carriers who hold their own authority.

Each of these has paid plans with different levels of access. We compare features in more detail in our guide to [the best load boards for owner-operators](/blog/best-load-boards-for-owner-operators.html).

## Free load boards: what to expect

A free load board can be a good way to start, especially for a brand-new carrier with a tight budget. Free options include free tiers of some boards and brokers' own load portals.

Typical trade-offs:

- Fewer loads and less coverage of some lanes
- Fewer search filters
- Little or no broker credit and payment data
- More competition for each posted load

If you use a free board, take extra care checking the broker before you book.

## How carriers use a load board, step by step

1. **Set your search.** Choose your equipment, empty location, search radius and date.
2. **Set the destination.** Search to a specific market, a region, or anywhere, depending on your plans.
3. **Sort and filter.** Look at deadhead to pickup, total miles and rate. Hide brokers you will not work with.
4. **Check the broker.** Look up its MC number on FMCSA, and review credit score and days to pay if the board shows them.
5. **Call or message.** Confirm the details and ask about anything missing: appointment times, commodity, tarps, lumpers, detention policy.
6. **Negotiate.** Use market data and your own cost per mile to counter.
7. **Book and get the rate confirmation.** Read it before pickup.

## How to judge a posted load

The posted rate per loaded mile is not the whole story. What matters is the rate on **every** mile you drive, including empty miles to the pickup.

**Worked example: two dry van posts (assumptions labelled)**

- **Load A:** $2,400 for 700 loaded miles, 90 miles deadhead
- **Load B:** $2,250 for 680 loaded miles, 15 miles deadhead

Rate per loaded mile:

- Load A: $2,400 / 700 = **$3.43**
- Load B: $2,250 / 680 = **$3.31**

Rate per total mile:

- Load A: $2,400 / (700 + 90) = $2,400 / 790 = **$3.04**
- Load B: $2,250 / (680 + 15) = $2,250 / 695 = **$3.24**

Load B pays less on paper but more on every mile driven. Then ask where each load leaves you. A slightly lower rate into a strong freight market can beat a high rate into a dead one. ## Load board safety tips

Fraud is a real problem on open marketplaces. Protect yourself:

- Verify the broker's MC number and contact details independently
- Be cautious if the phone number or email does not match the company's official records
- Never pay anyone to "release" a load
- Do not share login details for your load board, ELD or factoring account
- Watch for double brokering signs, such as a load that suddenly changes hands

## Limits of load boards

Load boards are useful, but they have limits:

- **Competition.** Many carriers see the same post, which can push rates down.
- **Time.** Searching and calling can take hours a day, often while you should be driving or resting.
- **Spot only.** Most posts are one-off loads, not repeat lanes.

That is why many carriers combine boards with broker relationships and direct freight. See [how to find loads for your truck](/blog/how-to-find-loads-for-your-truck.html) for a broader plan.
