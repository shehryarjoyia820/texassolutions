Choosing a **factoring company** is one of the most important money decisions an owner-operator makes. The right one keeps cash moving and helps you avoid bad brokers. The wrong one can drain margins with hidden fees and lock you into a contract that is hard to leave. This guide compares factoring by features and gives you the questions to ask.

If you are new to factoring, start with our guide to [freight factoring for owner-operators](/blog/freight-factoring-explained.html), then come back here to compare offers.

## Recourse vs non-recourse

This is the first big choice.

- **Recourse factoring.** If the broker does not pay within a set period, you buy back the invoice or it is deducted from future funding. Usually lower rates.
- **Non-recourse factoring.** The factoring company absorbs the loss if a broker cannot pay, usually only for credit reasons like insolvency. Usually higher rates.

Ask exactly what "non-recourse" covers. In many contracts it does not cover disputes, short pays, claims or missing paperwork. Those come back to you either way.

## Know the real cost per invoice

The headline percentage is only part of the cost. Ask for every fee in writing.

### Fees to ask about

- Factoring rate, and whether it rises the longer an invoice stays unpaid
- Same-day funding, wire or ACH fees
- Fuel advance fees
- Application, setup or onboarding fees
- Monthly minimum or low-volume fees
- Invoice processing or document fees
- Credit check fees
- Termination or early exit fees
- Fees for sending paper mail or originals

### Worked example

Hypothetical example comparing two offers on $20,000 of monthly invoices from 8 loads:

| Item | Offer A | Offer B |
|---|---|---|
| Rate | 2.5% = $500 | 3.0% = $600 |
| Funding fee per transfer | $15 x 8 = $120 | Included |
| Monthly minimum fee | $50 | None |
| Total monthly cost | **$670** | **$600** |

Offer A looks cheaper but costs more once fees are added. Always calculate total cost per month for your real volume. Then plug factoring cost into your [cost per mile calculator](/cost-per-mile-calculator.html) so it is reflected in the rates you accept.

## Advance rate and reserve

Some factoring companies advance a portion of the invoice up front and hold the rest as a reserve, released when the broker pays (minus fees). Others advance nearly the full amount minus the fee.

Ask:

- What percent is advanced?
- How long is the reserve held, and when is it released?
- Can the reserve be used to cover chargebacks on other invoices?

A lower reserve means more cash in hand, but compare it against the total fees.

## Contract terms and how you leave

Many carriers find out about contract terms only when they try to leave. Read these sections carefully:

- **Contract length** and whether it auto-renews
- **Notice period** required to cancel, and how notice must be given
- **Exit fees** or liquidated damages
- **Full-turnover requirement**: must you factor every invoice from approved customers?
- **UCC-1 filing**: what it covers and how fast it is released
- **Notice of assignment (NOA)**: how and when the NOA is released so brokers pay you directly again

Have an attorney or trusted advisor review the agreement before you sign. If anything is unclear, ask for the explanation in writing.

## Funding speed and process

Cash flow is why you factor, so test the process:

- How fast are invoices funded after submission?
- What is the daily cutoff time, and do they fund on weekends or holidays?
- How do you submit paperwork: mobile app, scan, email?
- Which documents are required? Usually a signed rate con, signed BOL or POD, and your invoice.
- How are lumper receipts and accessorials handled?

Our guides on [how to read a rate confirmation](/blog/how-to-read-a-rate-confirmation.html) and [TONU, layover and lumper pay](/blog/tonu-layover-and-lumper-pay.html) explain the paperwork that makes funding smooth.

## Broker credit tools

A good factoring company helps you avoid bad payers. Ask:

- Can I check a broker's credit before I book, and is it instant?
- Do you share days-to-pay data?
- What happens if I haul for a broker you do not approve?
- Do you alert me to fraud warnings or brokers with payment problems?

Credit checks are not a substitute for your own vetting. See [how to vet a freight broker](/blog/how-to-vet-a-freight-broker.html).

## Support and extras

- Is there a real person to call, and during what hours?
- Is there a dedicated account rep?
- Do they offer fuel discounts or fuel advances, and at what cost?
- Do they provide reporting for your bookkeeping?
- How do they handle collections, and how do they treat your broker relationships?

Extras are only worth it if you would use them and the core terms are fair.

## Factoring company checklist

Before you sign, confirm you have:

1. Every fee in writing and a calculated monthly total at your volume
2. A clear explanation of recourse and what non-recourse really covers
3. The advance rate and reserve terms
4. Contract length, renewal, notice period and exit costs
5. Full-turnover requirements, if any
6. UCC and NOA release process
7. Funding times and cutoffs
8. Broker credit check access
9. A support contact you have actually spoken to
10. Your own reviewer's read on the contract
