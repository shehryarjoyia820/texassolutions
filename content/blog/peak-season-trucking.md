The last three months of the year are the busiest stretch for many carriers. **Peak season trucking loads** come from retail restocking, holiday food and grocery, and e-commerce. For an owner-operator, Q4 can be the best quarter of the year or a frustrating one stuck in the wrong market. This guide shows how to plan **holiday freight loads**, which regions to watch, and how to keep deadhead down when everyone is busy.

## What drives Q4 freight

- **Retail restocking.** Stores and warehouses fill shelves from late summer through November.
- **Holiday food.** Turkey, meat, dairy, baking goods and frozen food move hard before Thanksgiving and Christmas. Reefer demand climbs.
- **E-commerce.** Parcel and fulfillment networks need extra capacity, which pulls dry van, power only and box truck freight.
- **Year-end shipping.** Some shippers move freight before the year closes.

Then, in the last week or so of December, freight drops off as receivers close. Plan for both.

## Reefer rates per mile in peak season

Reefer is usually the first trailer to feel the holiday rush. ## Regional picture: Texas, Midwest and California loads

### Texas loads

Dallas-Fort Worth and Houston are national distribution hubs, and Laredo is the busiest cross-border crossing. Q4 brings retail, grocery and cross-border freight. Houston also stays busy with energy and construction freight for hotshots and flatbeds. See [Texas truck dispatch](/texas-truck-dispatch.html).

### Midwest loads

Chicago, Indianapolis, Columbus and Kansas City host large warehouse and rail networks. Midwest loads in Q4 include retail, food processing and the end of the harvest. Winter weather can slow everything, so build in time. See [Illinois truck dispatch](/truck-dispatch/illinois.html), [Ohio](/truck-dispatch/ohio.html) and [Indiana](/truck-dispatch/indiana.html).

### California loads

The Southern California ports and Inland Empire warehouses push import freight inland for the holidays. California loads often pay well outbound; the challenge is getting back in without a long empty run. See [California truck dispatch](/truck-dispatch/california.html).

For other states, use our [truck dispatch by state](/truck-dispatch-by-state.html) list.

## Seven ways to make the most of peak season

1. **Position early.** Be near a strong outbound market before the rush, not after.
2. **Book the reload first.** Before you accept a load, check freight out of the delivery city.
3. **Negotiate harder on short-notice loads.** Brokers with uncovered holiday freight have more room.
4. **Watch deadhead.** A high rate with 200 empty miles may pay less than a normal load next door. Use the [deadhead miles calculator](/deadhead-miles-calculator.html).
5. **Plan for waiting.** Warehouses are busy; log check-in times and claim detention. Try the [detention pay calculator](/detention-pay-calculator.html).
6. **Keep the truck ready.** A breakdown in December costs more than any rate gain. Budget with the [maintenance calculator](/maintenance-budget-calculator.html).
7. **Set home time now.** Pick your dates and plan the last loads so you end near home.

## Watch your hours

Peak season tempts long days. The hours-of-service limits do not change for the holidays. Plan loads you can run legally with the [hours of service calculator](/hours-of-service-calculator.html).
