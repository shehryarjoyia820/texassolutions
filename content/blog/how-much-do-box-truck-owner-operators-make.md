"How much can I make with a box truck?" is usually the first question people ask, and the honest answer is that it depends on three things: how many miles you run, what those miles pay, and what it costs you to run them. Gross revenue gets the attention, but net income is what you live on.

This post breaks down box truck income with a worked example. For the semi side of the same question, see [how much owner-operators make](/blog/how-much-do-owner-operators-make.html).

## Gross vs net: the difference that matters

- **Gross** is what the loads pay before any expenses.
- **Net** is what is left after fuel, insurance, payments, repairs, fees and other costs.
- **Take-home** is net minus income and self-employment taxes.

Two drivers with the same weekly gross can take home very different amounts if one has a paid-off truck and low insurance and the other has a big payment and runs a lot of empty miles.

## What box truck loads pay

As a rough guide, box truck loads often pay about **$1.80 to $3.20 per mile**. Rates move with:

- **Lane.** Loads into areas with little outbound freight need to pay more to cover the trip out.
- **Season.** Retail peaks and holiday volume can push rates up; slow months push them down.
- **Urgency and service.** Expedited, team or liftgate-required freight often pays more.
- **Distance.** Short loads often pay more per mile but take more time per dollar.

Compare equipment types on our [rate per mile guide](/truck-dispatch-rates.html). Rates are market-driven and not guaranteed.

## Example weekly gross for box trucks (hypothetical)

As a hypothetical example, OTR box trucks might gross about **$7,000 to $9,000 a week**. That is not a promise. Weeks with breakdowns, home time, weather or soft freight markets will come in lower, and some weeks may come in higher.

## Worked example: a good week

Here is a sample week with clearly labelled assumptions. Replace them with your own figures.

**Assumptions:**

- Weekly gross: **$8,000**
- Loaded miles: 3,000, empty (deadhead) miles: 400, total 3,400
- Average fuel economy: 9.5 mpg (26 ft diesel, mix of loaded and empty)
- Diesel price: $4.50 a gallon
- Dispatch: 8% of gross
- Factoring: 3% of gross
- Insurance: $350 a week
- Truck payment: $450 a week
- Maintenance and tire reserve: $0.15 a mile
- ELD, phone, tolls and other: $100 a week

**Costs:**

- Fuel: 3,400 / 9.5 = 357.9 gallons x $4.50 = **$1,611**
- Dispatch: $8,000 x 0.08 = **$640**
- Factoring: $8,000 x 0.03 = **$240**
- Insurance: **$350**
- Truck payment: **$450**
- Maintenance reserve: 3,400 x $0.15 = **$510**
- Other: **$100**
- **Total: $3,901**

**Net before taxes:** $8,000 - $3,901 = **$4,099**, or about 51% of gross.

## Worked example: a slow week

Same truck, same fixed costs, fewer miles and lower rates.

**Assumptions:** weekly gross **$5,000**, total miles 2,200, other assumptions unchanged.

- Fuel: 2,200 / 9.5 = 231.6 gallons x $4.50 = **$1,042**
- Dispatch: **$400**
- Factoring: **$150**
- Insurance: **$350**
- Truck payment: **$450**
- Maintenance reserve: 2,200 x $0.15 = **$330**
- Other: **$100**
- **Total: $2,822**

**Net before taxes:** $5,000 - $2,822 = **$2,178**, or about 44% of gross.

Notice that fixed costs did not shrink when revenue dropped. That is why slow weeks hurt more than the drop in gross suggests, and why a cash reserve matters.

## Turning weekly numbers into a yearly estimate

Do not multiply your best week by 52. A more realistic method:

1. Estimate how many weeks you will actually work. Home time, repairs and holidays count against you.
2. Use an average week, not your best week.
3. Subtract taxes. Set aside money each week for quarterly estimated payments and talk with a tax professional.

For example, if you average **$3,000 net a week** (an assumption between the two examples above) and work **46 weeks**, that is 3,000 x 46 = **$138,000 before taxes**. If your average is lower, or you work fewer weeks, the annual figure drops quickly.

## The costs that decide box truck income

### Fuel

A 26 ft diesel box truck commonly gets somewhere around 8.5 to 11 mpg, lower when heavy. At box truck rates, fuel can take a large share of every dollar. Check your fuel cost by state with the [truck fuel cost calculator](/truck-fuel-cost-calculator.html?truck=box-truck#fuelCalc).

### Empty miles

Every empty mile costs fuel and wear and pays nothing. Planning the next load before you deliver is one of the simplest ways to raise net income. See [what deadhead is and how to reduce it](/blog/what-is-deadhead-in-trucking.html).

### Insurance and payments

These do not change with how much you run. The more miles you spread them over, the lower your cost per mile.

### Unpaid extras

Detention, layovers and lumper charges add up. If you do not document and invoice them, you are working for free. Texas Solutions handles detention and lumper collection as part of its [back office services](/trucking-back-office-services.html).
