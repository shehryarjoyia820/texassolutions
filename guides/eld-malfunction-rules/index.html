ELD Malfunction Rules: What to Do When Your ELD Fails

By Texas Solutions Dispatch Team · October 21, 2026 · 5 min read

Compliance ELD
Quick answer

What do you do when your ELD malfunctions?

Note the malfunction, notify your motor carrier in writing within 24 hours, and switch to paper logs, reconstructing the current day and the previous 7 days if the ELD cannot show them. The carrier generally has 8 days to repair or replace the device unless FMCSA grants an extension. Confirm current details in FMCSA's ELD rule (49 CFR 395.34).

An ELD that freezes, loses its connection to the engine or stops recording can stop your day in its tracks if you do not know the ELD malfunction rules. The good news is that the federal rule gives drivers a clear procedure, so a broken device does not have to mean a violation or a parked truck.

This guide explains what counts as a malfunction, what the driver must do, what the carrier must do, and how to stay ready before anything goes wrong. The rule lives in 49 CFR 395.34. Always confirm the current text with FMCSA, since guidance and interpretations can change.

What counts as an ELD malfunction

Registered ELDs are required to monitor themselves and flag problems. Problems fall into two groups:

  • Malfunctions. The device cannot reliably do one of its core jobs. The rule lists malfunction types such as power compliance, engine synchronization, timing, positioning, data recording, data transfer and "other" malfunctions identified by the manufacturer.
  • Data diagnostic events. Something is wrong with the data, but the device may still be working. Examples include power data diagnostics, engine synchronization diagnostics, missing required data elements, data transfer issues and unidentified driving records.

Most ELDs show an "M" indicator for a malfunction and a "D" indicator for a data diagnostic event, along with a code. Your ELD user manual explains what each code means for your specific device. A data diagnostic event usually means you should fix the data (for example, accept or reject unidentified driving, or re-pair a tablet), while a malfunction triggers the formal procedure below.

Driver steps when the ELD malfunctions

When the device shows a malfunction, or you notice it is not recording properly, follow these steps:

  1. Note the malfunction. Write down the date, time, what happened and any code shown on the screen. A photo of the screen is a useful backup.
  2. Notify your motor carrier in writing within 24 hours. A text, email or message through your company's system creates a record. If you are leased on, this goes to the carrier whose authority you run under.
  3. Reconstruct your records. If the ELD cannot display your logs, recreate your records of duty status on paper for the current 24-hour period and the previous 7 consecutive days. Skip any days you can still pull from the device or another source.
  4. Keep paper logs until the ELD is fixed. Continue on graph-grid paper logs (or another method allowed by the rule) until the device is repaired or replaced and working again.
  5. Show the inspector what you have. At a roadside inspection, explain the malfunction, show your notice to the carrier and present your paper logs.

What to keep in the cab at all times

The rule requires an ELD information packet in the truck. Use this as a quick checklist:

  • [ ] ELD user manual (paper or electronic)
  • [ ] Instruction sheet explaining how to transfer data to an inspector
  • [ ] Instruction sheet explaining malfunction reporting and recordkeeping
  • [ ] Blank paper graph-grid logs for at least 8 days
  • [ ] A pen and a copy of your recent hours, if you can print or save them

Missing instruction sheets or blank logs is an easy violation to avoid. Check the packet each time you do a pre-trip inspection or change trucks.

Carrier steps and the repair deadline

The motor carrier also has duties once it learns about a malfunction:

  • Repair or replace the ELD within 8 days of discovering the problem or receiving the driver's notice, whichever comes first.
  • Make sure drivers keep paper records in the meantime.
  • Request an extension if needed. If the carrier cannot fix the problem in time, it can ask FMCSA for an extension. The request generally goes to the FMCSA Division Administrator for the carrier's principal place of business, within 5 days after the driver's notice, with an explanation of the problem and the plan to fix it.

Keep copies of the driver's notice, repair or replacement records and any extension request. These are the first things an auditor will ask for if a malfunction shows up during an investigation.

A simple timeline example

Here is a hypothetical example of how this plays out:

Day What happens
Monday 2 p.m. ELD shows an engine sync malfunction. Driver notes the time and code and takes a photo.
Monday 3 p.m. Driver emails the carrier about the malfunction (well inside 24 hours).
Monday 3 p.m. Driver reconstructs the day and the prior 7 days on paper, since the tablet cannot display them.
Tuesday to Thursday Driver runs on paper logs. Carrier orders a replacement cable.
Friday New cable installed, ELD reconnects, driver goes back to electronic logs. Carrier files the repair record.

The device was repaired 4 days after the notice, inside the 8-day window. If the part had been backordered, the carrier would have needed to request an extension before the deadline rather than letting the driver run on paper indefinitely.

Common causes and quick fixes

Many "malfunctions" are connection problems you can solve in minutes:

  • Loose or damaged cable at the diagnostic port. Reseat it and check for bent pins.
  • Bluetooth or tablet pairing lost. Restart the app and the tablet, then re-pair.
  • Outdated app or firmware. Update on Wi-Fi before trips, not at a weigh station.
  • Power issues after a jump start, battery swap or wiring work.
  • Poor GPS signal in terminals or tunnels, which often clears on its own.

If a quick fix works and the indicator clears, note what happened anyway. If it does not clear, treat it as a malfunction and start the procedure. Never unplug the device to avoid recording time. Tampering is a serious violation and can lead to penalties for both the driver and the carrier.

How malfunctions affect your trip planning

Paper logs do not change your hours of service limits. You still have the same 11-hour driving limit, 14-hour window and 60/70-hour cycle. They do mean more work at every stop and more scrutiny at inspections, so build in a little extra time. Our hours of service rules guide covers the limits, and the hours of service calculator helps you plan a day by hand when the ELD is down.

Frequently asked questions

Can I keep driving if my ELD malfunctions?

Generally yes, as long as you follow the malfunction procedure: notify your carrier, keep paper records of duty status and stay within hours of service. You cannot simply drive with no record at all.

What is the difference between a malfunction and a data diagnostic event?

A malfunction means the device is failing at a core job, such as recording time, location or engine data. A data diagnostic event is a data problem, like missing data elements or unidentified driving, that usually needs attention but does not always mean the ELD has failed.

Does an owner-operator with their own authority have to notify themselves?

If you are your own motor carrier, you still need to document the malfunction, keep a written record of when it happened and what you did, and get the ELD fixed or replaced within the allowed time. Keep that record with your logs.

How many days of blank paper logs must I carry?

The rule requires a supply of blank driver's record of duty status graph-grids for at least 8 days. Keep them in the cab with your ELD instruction sheets.
