Crossing Into Canada and Mexico: A Trucker's Guide

By Texas Solutions Dispatch Team · October 21, 2026 · 5 min read

Compliance Cross-border
Quick answer

What does a US truck driver need to haul into Canada?

You need a valid passport or other accepted travel document such as a FAST card, a clean admissibility record, a carrier code and a way to file Canada's advance eManifest, insurance that covers Canada, and your IFTA and IRP credentials set up for the provinces you will run. Canada also has its own hours of service and ELD rules. Confirm current requirements with CBSA, CBP and your insurance agent before booking.

Cross-border trucking can open up good-paying lanes, especially into Canada and to the Texas-Mexico border, but it adds paperwork that domestic loads never touch. A missing manifest or the wrong document can leave you parked at the border for hours. Here is what a US owner-operator needs to know before taking the first cross-border load.

Is cross-border right for you?

It fits best if you:

  • Run near the northern border or the major Texas crossings
  • Have a clean driving and criminal record
  • Can handle a few extra filings per load, or work with someone who can
  • Have insurance that will cover the trip

If you mostly run the Southeast, cross-border may not be worth the setup. If you are based in the upper Midwest, Northeast or South Texas, it can add steady freight.

Driver documents

For Canada:

  • A passport, or another accepted document such as a FAST card
  • A valid CDL and medical certificate (the US and Canada recognize each other's commercial licenses for this purpose)
  • A clean admissibility history. Criminal convictions, including impaired driving, can make a driver inadmissible to Canada.

For Mexico:

  • A passport if you actually enter Mexico
  • In practice, most US drivers only go as far as a US border yard or warehouse

Rules on documents and admissibility change. Check with US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) and the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) before your first trip.

Carrier setup

Before you haul the first load, your company typically needs:

Item What it is
SCAC code A carrier identifier used in US customs filings.
CBSA carrier code Canada's carrier identifier for advance manifests.
eManifest access A way to file advance manifests with both countries, either directly or through a service provider.
US import user fee decal An annual fee paid by commercial trucks entering the US.
IFTA and IRP Canadian provinces take part in IFTA and IRP, so add the provinces you will run to your IRP account and track miles by jurisdiction. See our IFTA guide.
Insurance Confirm your policy covers Canadian operations. US policies generally do not cover driving in Mexico. Read trucking insurance explained.
Bond (if needed) Some in-bond moves require a bonded carrier. Ask your customs broker.

Advance eManifest: the step people miss

Both countries require advance electronic manifests from highway carriers:

  • Into Canada: an ACI eManifest filed with CBSA before arrival, generally at least an hour before you reach the border.
  • Into the US: an ACE eManifest filed with CBP before arrival, generally at least an hour before, with a shorter window for some FAST shipments.

The shipper or consignee usually uses a customs broker to clear the goods themselves. Your job is to make sure the broker has the shipment information and that your manifest references it correctly. Expect barcoded cover sheets or reference numbers that tie the shipment to the customs entry.

Step by step for a typical load into Canada:

  1. Get the commercial invoice and the customs broker's name and contact from the shipper.
  2. Confirm the broker has received the documents and is ready to clear.
  3. File the ACI eManifest with the correct carrier code, driver, truck, trailer and shipment details.
  4. Print or keep the shipment reference sheet and documents in the cab.
  5. Arrive at the port of entry you declared, and answer the officer's questions directly.
  6. For the return trip, repeat the process for the US side with an ACE eManifest.

Exact timing windows and filing methods change. Confirm them with CBSA and CBP.

Cabotage: no domestic moves

Canada restricts foreign carriers from hauling domestic freight from one Canadian point to another, and the US has similar limits on foreign carriers. The narrow exceptions are tightly defined. Practically, Canadian work means: load in the US, deliver in Canada, then reload back to the US. Plan the reload before you cross. Our guide on planning a profitable round trip covers how to price both legs together.

Hours of service and ELDs in Canada

Canada has its own federal hours of service rules, and they differ from US rules. As a general picture, Canada uses a daily limit of 13 hours of driving and 14 hours on duty, a 16-hour elapsed work shift limit, and two cycle options (70 hours in 7 days or 120 hours in 14 days). Canada also requires electronic logging devices certified for Canadian use.

When you are in Canada, you follow Canadian rules; when you return, you follow US rules, and your recent hours carry over. Make sure your ELD supports both rule sets and that you know how to switch. Review the US hours of service rules and confirm Canadian details with Transport Canada.

Hauling to Mexico: the border handoff

Most US-Mexico freight moves in stages:

  1. A US carrier hauls the trailer to a border city such as Laredo, El Paso or McAllen.
  2. A transfer (drayage) carrier takes the trailer across the bridge through both customs processes.
  3. A Mexican carrier handles the inland leg in Mexico, and the reverse happens northbound.

For a US owner-operator, this means most Mexico freight looks like a domestic load to or from a border warehouse. The things to watch:

  • Long wait times at border yards; negotiate detention up front.
  • Trailer interchange: confirm who is responsible for your trailer while it is in Mexico if it crosses.
  • Mexican liability insurance is required for any US truck operating in Mexico.

Border-day tips

  • Keep documents organized in one folder, in the order officers ask for them.
  • Do not carry firearms, cannabis or other prohibited items. Rules on these differ sharply between countries.
  • Budget time. A smooth crossing can be quick; a document problem can take hours.
  • Be honest and direct with officers. Do not joke.

Cross-border rules, fees and programs change often. Confirm current requirements with CBP, CBSA, Transport Canada, FMCSA and your customs broker, and talk to your insurance agent before your first trip.

Frequently asked questions

Can a DUI keep me out of Canada?

It can. Canada treats impaired driving seriously, and a past conviction can make a person inadmissible unless they qualify for rehabilitation or a temporary permit. Check with Canadian immigration authorities well before you book a Canadian load.

Can I pick up a load in Canada and deliver it elsewhere in Canada?

Generally no. Cabotage rules restrict foreign drivers and trucks from moving domestic freight between two points inside Canada, with only narrow exceptions. Plan Canadian work as loads into Canada with a reload back to the US.

Can I drive my US truck deep into Mexico?

Most US owner-operators do not. Freight usually changes hands at the border, with a transfer carrier crossing and a Mexican carrier handling the inland leg. Running in Mexico needs Mexican permits and Mexican insurance.

What is a FAST card?

FAST is a trusted traveler program for commercial drivers run jointly by US and Canadian border agencies. It can speed processing, and the FAST lanes are available when the carrier and the shipment also meet program requirements.
