Lease vs Buy a Truck: Which Is Better for Owner-Operators?

By Texas Solutions Dispatch Team · October 21, 2026 · 4 min read

Equipment Finance
Quick answer

Is it better to lease or buy a semi truck?

Buying usually costs less over the long run and builds equity, but you carry repair risk and need more cash upfront. Leasing can lower upfront cost and, with full-service leases, bundle maintenance, but you build little or no equity and must follow the lease terms. The right choice depends on your cash, credit, miles and how long you plan to keep the truck.

The lease vs buy a truck decision shapes your cash flow, your repair risk and how much your business is worth in five years. Neither choice is always right. This guide explains the main options, what each one costs in practice, and how to compare them with a simple hypothetical example. It is general education, so have a CPA and, for contracts, an attorney review your specific offers.

The main ways to get a truck

  • Buy with cash. You own it outright. No payment, but your cash is tied up in the truck.
  • Buy with a loan. You own it, the lender holds a lien until it is paid off. See how to finance a semi truck.
  • Lease from a leasing company. You pay to use the truck for a set term. Some leases include maintenance (often called full-service), others do not. Some are structured with a set end value you may need to cover.
  • Lease-purchase through a carrier. You lease a truck from or through a carrier, usually while hauling under that carrier, with a path to ownership at the end.

Each version has many variations, so always ask for the full contract, not a summary.

Buying: pros and cons

Pros - Usually the lowest total cost if you keep the truck for years - You build equity and can sell or trade the truck later - Freedom to choose your shop, your specs and how you run - Once paid off, your cost per mile drops sharply

Cons - Bigger upfront cash: down payment, taxes, registration - You pay for every repair, including major engine and aftertreatment work - Resale value is uncertain - Credit requirements can be tough for new businesses

Leasing: pros and cons

Pros - Lower upfront cash in many cases - Full-service leases can bundle maintenance and sometimes a replacement truck during repairs - Predictable monthly costs - Easier to move into newer equipment at the end of the term

Cons - Usually a higher total cost over time - Little or no equity at the end, depending on the lease type - Mileage limits, condition rules and early termination fees - With some carrier lease-purchase deals, the same company controls your freight and your truck, which can limit your choices

Questions to ask before signing any lease

  1. What is the total amount I will pay over the full term?
  2. Is there a mileage cap, and what does each extra mile cost?
  3. What maintenance is included, and what is excluded (tires, aftertreatment, accidents)?
  4. Who pays for repairs when the truck is down, and is there a replacement unit?
  5. What happens if I end the lease early, or if I am injured or sick?
  6. What do I owe at the end, and can I buy the truck? At what price?
  7. For carrier lease-purchase: am I required to haul only for this carrier, how is my pay calculated, and what deductions come out of settlements?
  8. Can I have an attorney review the contract before I sign?

If a company will not give you time to review the contract, treat that as a warning sign.

Worked example: three-year cost comparison

All numbers below are hypothetical and simplified to show how to compare. Real offers will differ.

Item (36 months) Buy with loan Full-service lease
Upfront cash $20,000 down payment $5,000 deposit
Monthly payment $2,100 $3,000
Payments over 36 months $75,600 $108,000
Repairs and maintenance (estimated) $27,000 $6,000 (items not covered)
Total cash out $122,600 $119,000
Equity at month 36 (estimated truck value minus loan balance) $30,000 $0
Net cost after equity about $92,600 about $119,000

In this example, leasing uses less cash up front and has steadier costs, but buying ends up about $26,000 cheaper once you count equity. Change any input, such as a big unexpected engine repair or a weak resale market, and the answer can flip. Use the truck loan calculator and maintenance budget calculator to plug in your own numbers.

Convert both options to cost per mile

Whatever you choose, the payment has to fit your freight. Divide the monthly payment plus expected repairs by your realistic monthly miles. At a hypothetical 9,000 miles a month:

  • Buy: ($2,100 + $750 repairs) / 9,000 = about $0.32 per mile
  • Lease: ($3,000 + about $167 uncovered items) / 9,000 = about $0.35 per mile

Then add that figure to fuel, insurance and your other costs in the cost per mile calculator. If your rates do not cover it with room for profit, neither option works yet.

Which one fits you

Buying often fits when you: - Have a cash reserve for repairs, not just a down payment - Plan to keep the truck for several years - Have mechanical knowledge or a trusted shop - Want full control over where and how you run

Leasing often fits when you: - Have limited cash but steady freight - Want predictable costs and less repair risk - Prefer to cycle into newer equipment - Have read and understood every term of the contract

Frequently asked questions

What is a lease-purchase program?

It is an arrangement, often offered through a carrier, where a driver leases a truck with the option or obligation to buy it at the end. Terms vary widely, so read the contract carefully, understand who controls your loads and pay, and have an attorney review it.

Are lease payments tax deductible?

Lease payments on business equipment are often treated differently from loan payments for tax purposes, and the details depend on the lease type. Ask a CPA how a specific lease would be treated before you sign.

Can I lease a truck with bad credit?

Some leasing options are easier to qualify for than loans, but weaker credit usually means a higher cost or larger deposit. Compare the total cost over the full term against financing a less expensive truck.
