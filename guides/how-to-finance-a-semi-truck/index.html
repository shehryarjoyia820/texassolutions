How to Finance a Semi Truck: A Step-by-Step Guide

By Texas Solutions Dispatch Team · October 21, 2026 · 4 min read

Finance Equipment
Quick answer

How do you finance a semi truck?

Most owner-operators finance a semi with an equipment loan secured by the truck itself. Lenders look at your credit, time in business, driving experience, down payment and the truck's age and mileage. Get several written offers, compare the total cost over the full term, and make sure the payment fits your real cost per mile.

Learning how to finance a semi truck is mostly about understanding what lenders want and what the loan really costs over its full term. The right loan keeps your payment affordable on slow weeks; the wrong one can take over your business. This guide walks through the process step by step with a hypothetical example. It is general education, not financial advice, so review any offer with your CPA or a trusted advisor before you sign.

How semi truck loans work

Most truck financing is an equipment loan: the lender pays the seller, you make monthly payments, and the truck serves as collateral. If you stop paying, the lender can repossess it. The main terms to understand:

  • Price financed: purchase price plus any taxes, fees or warranties rolled into the loan, minus your down payment.
  • Interest rate (APR): what borrowing costs per year.
  • Term: how many months you will pay.
  • Monthly payment: the result of the three items above.
  • Fees and conditions: documentation fees, prepayment penalties, balloon payments, insurance requirements.

What lenders look at

Every lender weighs things differently, but most review:

  • Personal and business credit history, including late payments and recent collections
  • Time in business and time under your own authority
  • CDL experience, especially in the type of equipment you are buying
  • Down payment you can bring
  • The truck: age, mileage, make, condition and whether it suits the work
  • Cash reserves and other debts
  • Income history, such as bank statements, settlement sheets or tax returns

New authorities can usually still get financing, but expect tighter terms. Clean, up-to-date books help a lot.

Documents to have ready

Lenders move faster when your file is complete. Gather these before you apply:

  • Driver's license and CDL
  • Business formation papers and EIN letter, if you have a business entity
  • USDOT and MC numbers, if you already have authority
  • Recent business and personal bank statements
  • Tax returns or settlement statements showing income
  • The truck's details: year, make, model, VIN, mileage and the seller's quote
  • Proof of insurance or an insurance quote

Step by step: financing your truck

  1. Know your numbers first. Calculate your cost per mile and what payment the business can afford on a slow month.
  2. Check your credit and fix errors before you apply.
  3. Save your down payment and a reserve. Plan for both. A truck with no repair fund is one breakdown away from a missed payment.
  4. Choose the truck carefully. If buying used, follow our used semi truck buying guide before you commit.
  5. Get several written offers from different types of lenders (banks, credit unions, equipment finance companies, dealer financing).
  6. Compare total cost, not only the monthly payment.
  7. Read every condition: prepayment penalties, late fees, required insurance, balloon payments.
  8. Have a CPA or advisor review the final offer before you sign.

Worked example: the same truck, two offers

Here is a hypothetical example. The numbers are made up to show the math, not to suggest real rates.

You are financing $80,000 over 60 months.

Offer A Offer B
APR 12% 18%
Monthly payment (approx.) $1,780 $2,030
Total paid over 60 months about $106,800 about $121,800
Total interest about $26,800 about $41,800

The $250 difference in monthly payment looks small, but over five years Offer B costs about $15,000 more. Run your own scenarios with the truck loan calculator.

Now convert the payment to a per-mile cost. If the truck runs a hypothetical 9,000 miles a month:

  • Offer A: $1,780 / 9,000 = about $0.20 per mile
  • Offer B: $2,030 / 9,000 = about $0.23 per mile

That per-mile figure goes straight into your break-even calculator. If a slow month drops you to 6,000 miles, Offer B becomes about $0.34 per mile. Always test the payment against a bad month, not your best month.

Common loan features to understand

Balloon payments

Some loans have lower monthly payments with a large lump sum due at the end. Make sure you know exactly how you would pay it.

Prepayment penalties

Some lenders charge a fee if you pay the loan off early. If you plan to refinance or sell, this matters.

Add-ons rolled into the loan

Extended warranties, GAP coverage and fees may be added to the amount financed. You then pay interest on them. Ask for an itemized breakdown.

Insurance requirements

Lenders usually require physical damage coverage naming them as loss payee. Get an insurance quote before you sign so the true monthly cost is clear.

Red flags in truck financing

  • Pressure to sign the same day
  • A payment quoted with no APR, term or total cost in writing
  • Requests to pay large upfront "approval fees" before any written offer
  • Terms that change between the quote and the final contract
  • A seller and lender who will not let you inspect the truck or review documents with an advisor

When something feels wrong, walk away. There is always another truck.

Financing vs leasing

Financing means you own the truck and build equity, but you carry repair risk. Leasing can mean lower upfront cash but different long-term costs. Read lease vs buy a truck to compare the two side by side.

Frequently asked questions

How much down payment do you need for a semi truck?

It varies by lender, credit and the truck. Stronger applicants may put down less, while new businesses, lower credit scores or older trucks usually require more money down. Get the requirement in writing from each lender you talk to.

Can I get a truck loan with a new MC number?

Some lenders work with new authorities, often asking for more down payment, a higher rate, or proof of CDL experience. A longer track record and clean financial records usually improve your options over time.

Is a longer loan term better?

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but usually increases the total interest paid, and on a used truck you may still owe money when major repairs start. Compare total cost, not just the payment.
