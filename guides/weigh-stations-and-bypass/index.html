Weigh Stations and Bypass: Weight Limits, Scales and Tips

By Texas Solutions Dispatch Team · October 21, 2026 · 4 min read

Compliance Weight limits
Quick answer

How do weigh stations and bypass programs work?

Weigh stations check that trucks meet weight, size, safety and credential rules, and officers may pull trucks in for inspection. Bypass programs use a transponder or phone app to screen eligible trucks on the approach and may signal you to pass if your safety record and credentials qualify. You must still follow posted signs and pull in whenever directed.

Weigh stations are a normal part of every trucker's day, but a scale ticket or a surprise inspection can cost you hours and money. Knowing the weight limits, how to load legally and how bypass programs work makes scales a quick stop instead of a problem.

This guide covers the basics as we understand them. Weight and size rules vary by state and change over time, so confirm current limits with FMCSA, the Federal Highway Administration and each state you run.

What happens at a weigh station

Weigh stations, sometimes called ports of entry or inspection stations, are run by states. Officers check:

  • Weight: gross vehicle weight and individual axle weights
  • Size: height, width and length when needed
  • Credentials: registration, IFTA, operating authority and permits
  • Safety: obvious defects, and sometimes a full roadside inspection
  • Driver items: license, medical card and hours of service

Most stations weigh you as you roll slowly across the scale. Signals or signs then tell you to continue, pull around back or park and bring in your paperwork. Read the signs carefully; missing a "report to scale house" signal can turn a quick stop into a citation. For what happens if you are pulled in, see our guide to DOT roadside inspections.

Federal weight limits

On the Interstate System, the general federal limits are:

Limit Federal maximum
Gross vehicle weight 80,000 lb
Single axle 20,000 lb
Tandem axle 34,000 lb
Axle groups Governed by the federal bridge formula

The bridge formula limits how much weight can sit on a group of axles based on how many axles there are and how far apart they are. It is why axle spacing matters, not just total weight.

States can have different limits on non-interstate roads, and some states allow heavier weights through permits or older grandfather rules. Many states also set rules for steer axle weight and kingpin-to-rear-axle distance. If you need to haul heavier or wider freight, see our guide to oversize and overweight permits.

Some states allow a small enforcement tolerance, but do not plan around it. Load to the legal limit.

Who has to stop

Each state decides which vehicles must enter an open station. The threshold is often based on gross vehicle weight rating or vehicle type, and it can include smaller trucks like hotshots and box trucks. Do not assume you can skip a scale because you drive a pickup with a trailer. Read the signs at the station entrance, which often state who must enter.

How bypass programs work

Bypass programs let qualified trucks skip some weigh stations without slowing down. They come in two main forms:

  • Transponder-based programs that use a device mounted on the windshield
  • Smartphone or in-cab app programs that use GPS and the phone or ELD tablet

As you approach a participating site, the system checks your vehicle, carrier and sometimes weigh-in-motion data. You then get a green signal (bypass) or red signal (pull in). Factors that affect bypass eligibility often include:

  • Your carrier's safety record, including inspection and violation history
  • Whether your credentials are valid
  • Weigh-in-motion readings on approach, where available
  • Random selection, so even good carriers get pulled in sometimes

Questions to ask before you sign up for any bypass service:

  • [ ] Which states and sites does it cover on my lanes?
  • [ ] What does it cost per month, per truck or per bypass?
  • [ ] Does it work with my ELD tablet, or do I need separate hardware?
  • [ ] How does it handle toll payment, if at all?
  • [ ] Can I cancel without long contract fees?

A clean safety record is what earns bypasses over time. Our guide to CSA scores explained shows how inspections and violations feed into it.

Balancing your axle weights

You can be under 80,000 pounds and still be overweight on an axle. The fix is usually adjusting the trailer tandems or the fifth wheel.

Hypothetical example: A driver scales at a certified truck scale before heading out:

  • Steer: 11,800 lb
  • Drives: 32,600 lb
  • Trailer tandems: 35,100 lb
  • Gross: 79,500 lb

Gross is legal, but the trailer tandem is 1,100 pounds over the 34,000-pound limit. Sliding the trailer tandems toward the rear of the trailer shifts weight forward onto the drive axles. After a slide and a re-weigh:

  • Steer: 11,900 lb
  • Drives: 33,800 lb
  • Trailer tandems: 33,800 lb
  • Gross: 79,500 lb

Every axle group is now legal. How much weight moves per hole depends on the trailer, so re-weigh after each adjustment instead of guessing. Sliding the fifth wheel moves weight between the steer and the drive axles in the same way. Also check that your slide does not break a state's kingpin-to-axle rule.

If you cannot balance the load legally, the freight may need to be reworked at the shipper. Raise it before you leave, and note it on the bill of lading if possible.

Tips to get through scales smoothly

  1. Scale after loading at a certified scale near the shipper, and keep the ticket.
  2. Know your empty weights so you can estimate how much freight you can take.
  3. Ask the broker for accurate weight before booking. Our how to read a rate confirmation guide covers what to check.
  4. Keep paperwork organized: registration, IFTA license, permits, insurance, medical card and bill of lading.
  5. Do a quick walk-around at fuel stops; lights and tires are easy reasons to get pulled in.
  6. Follow every sign and signal, even if you have bypass.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to stop at a weigh station if it is open?

Generally yes, if your vehicle meets that state's weight or vehicle-type threshold, unless you receive a bypass signal from an authorized program. Thresholds differ by state, so read the signs and check each state's rules.

What is the federal gross weight limit for a semi?

On the Interstate System, the federal limit is generally 80,000 pounds gross, 20,000 pounds on a single axle and 34,000 pounds on a tandem axle, subject to the federal bridge formula. Permits and state grandfather rules can change this, so confirm with each state.

Does a bypass program guarantee I will skip the scale?

No. Bypass decisions are made each time, and some trucks are randomly pulled in even with a good record. Always follow the in-cab signal and roadside signs.

Can I be fined for skipping an open weigh station?

Yes. Failing to stop when required can lead to a citation and may lead to officers stopping you down the road. It can also prompt a closer inspection.
