Working With a Factoring Company: Setup, Paperwork and Payments

By Texas Solutions Dispatch Team · October 21, 2026 · 4 min read

Getting paid Factoring
Quick answer

How do you work with a factoring company day to day?

After onboarding, the factoring company sends a notice of assignment to your brokers so they pay the factor instead of you. For each load, you submit a clean packet (invoice, signed rate confirmation, signed BOL or POD and any receipts), get funded, and track which invoices are paid. Most problems come from missing paperwork, short pays and disputes, so a consistent routine matters.

Signing up is the easy part. Working with a factoring company day to day is where carriers either get fast, steady cash or end up chasing chargebacks and missing payments. This guide covers the routine that keeps funding smooth, from the first notice of assignment to switching companies if you ever need to.

If you are still comparing offers, read how to choose a factoring company first.

Onboarding: what to expect

Typical setup steps include:

  • An application with your company details, MC and DOT numbers
  • Copies of your operating authority, W-9, insurance certificate and bank details
  • A signed factoring agreement
  • A UCC-1 filing by the factor on your receivables
  • A list of brokers and shippers you work with, so the factor can check their credit

Keep a copy of everything you sign. Note your contract's renewal date and notice period in your calendar now, not when you want to leave.

The notice of assignment

The notice of assignment (NOA) is a letter from the factoring company telling a broker: "This carrier's invoices are assigned to us. Pay us, not the carrier."

  • The NOA is sent to each broker you invoice, usually when you set up with them or on your first factored load.
  • Many brokers want the NOA as part of your carrier packet. See the carrier packet checklist.
  • If a broker pays you directly by mistake, your contract almost always requires you to forward that payment to the factor. Report it immediately.

Submitting a clean invoice packet

Every factored load needs a complete packet. Missing or unclear documents are the number one cause of delays.

Standard packet

  1. Your invoice with load number, broker name, rate and any accessorials
  2. Signed rate confirmation that matches the invoice amount
  3. Signed BOL or proof of delivery, legible, with all pages
  4. Lumper receipts or other reimbursable receipts
  5. Detention, layover or TONU confirmation from the broker, if billed

Paperwork tips

  • Scan or photograph documents flat, in good light, with all corners visible
  • Submit the same day you deliver, before the factor's cutoff
  • Make sure the invoice total matches the rate con plus approved accessorials
  • Ask drivers to check that receivers signed and dated the BOL before leaving

Our guide to how to read a rate confirmation helps you catch mismatches before they cause funding problems.

Understand advances, reserves and fees

Read your funding statement for every batch. It should show:

  • Gross invoice amount
  • Advance paid
  • Factoring fee and any other fees
  • Reserve held, if any
  • Chargebacks or deductions

Worked example

Hypothetical example: you submit a $3,000 invoice. Your agreement advances 90% with a 3% fee, and releases the reserve when the broker pays.

  • Advance today: 90% x $3,000 = $2,700
  • Reserve held: $300
  • Fee: 3% x $3,000 = $90
  • When the broker pays in full, reserve released: $300 - $90 = $210
  • Total you receive: $2,700 + $210 = $2,910

If the broker short pays $100, that amount usually comes out of your reserve or next funding. Track it so you can dispute the short pay with the broker. Build the factoring fee into your per-mile math when you price loads.

Avoid chargebacks and short pays

Chargebacks happen when an invoice is not paid in full within the period in your agreement. Common causes and fixes:

Cause Prevention
Missing or unsigned POD Check signatures before leaving the receiver
Rate con and invoice mismatch Bill exactly what the rate con says
Accessorial not approved Get accessorials confirmed in writing first
Claim or damage dispute Note exceptions at pickup and delivery
Broker credit problem Run a credit check before every booking
Broker paid the carrier directly Make sure every broker has your NOA

Detention and TONU are frequent short-pay items. See how to get detention paid for documentation that holds up.

Use the credit check every time

Most factoring companies let you check a broker's credit before you book. Make it a rule: no credit check, no load. If the factor declines a broker, ask why. It may be slow payment, fraud warnings or a lack of history. Pair this with your own checks from how to vet a freight broker.

Keep your own records

Do not rely only on the factor's portal. Keep a simple ledger:

  • Load number, broker, invoice date and amount
  • Date funded and amount advanced
  • Date broker paid and reserve released
  • Any fees, chargebacks or short pays

Reconcile it weekly with your bank deposits. It also makes tax time and bookkeeping easier. If you would rather hand this off, our back office services can help keep records organized.

Switching or leaving a factoring company

If you decide to leave:

  1. Check your contract for notice period, how notice must be sent and any exit fees.
  2. Send written notice on time.
  3. Settle open invoices, chargebacks and any balance.
  4. Request a release letter for the NOA, to send to each broker.
  5. Confirm the UCC filing is terminated or amended.
  6. Send the new factor's NOA, or your own payment details, to brokers only after the release.

Brokers will keep paying the old factor until they receive a proper release, so do not skip steps 4 and 5. If the terms are confusing, talk to an attorney.

Frequently asked questions

Why did the broker pay me instead of my factoring company?

Usually the notice of assignment was not received or processed by that broker. Contact your factoring company right away, since contracts typically require you to forward any payment you receive on a factored invoice.

What is a chargeback in factoring?

A chargeback is when the factoring company takes back money it advanced, usually because the broker did not pay within a set time or paid less than invoiced. It is often deducted from your next funding or reserve.

Can I switch factoring companies?

Usually yes, once you follow your contract's notice period and settle any open balance. The old factor must release its notice of assignment and UCC filing so brokers pay the new one.
