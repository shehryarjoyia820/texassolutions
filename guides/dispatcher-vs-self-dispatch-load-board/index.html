Load Board vs Dispatcher: Should You Self-Dispatch or Hire a Truck Dispatcher?

By Texas Solutions Dispatch Team · October 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Dispatch Finding loads
Quick answer

Is it better to use a load board yourself or hire a truck dispatcher?

Self-dispatching from a load board saves the dispatch fee but costs hours every day in searching, calling brokers and paperwork. A dispatcher is worth it when better rates, fewer deadhead miles and the time you get back are worth more than the fee. Many owner-operators self-dispatch at first, then hire a dispatcher once phone time starts cutting into driving time.

Every owner-operator ends up asking the same question: load board vs dispatcher. Do you keep paying for a load board and book your own freight, or hand that work to a truck dispatcher for a percentage? There is no single right answer. This guide lays out the real costs of dispatcher vs self-dispatch so you can decide with your own numbers.

What self-dispatch really costs

Self-dispatch looks free apart from the board subscription. It is not. Here is where the cost hides:

  • Time. Searching boards, calling brokers, sending carrier packets, negotiating and checking rate confirmations easily takes a couple of hours a day. Those hours come out of driving time, sleep or home time.
  • Rate negotiation. Brokers negotiate all day. If you take the posted rate because you are tired or in a hurry, you leave money on the table on every load.
  • Deadhead. Booking one load at a time often means delivering into a market with no outbound freight and driving empty to the next one. See what deadhead is and how to reduce it.
  • Paperwork. Invoices, detention claims and lumper receipts get missed when you are also the driver.

What a dispatcher costs

A dispatcher usually charges a percentage of weekly gross. At Texas Solutions it is 5% for OTR semis, 7% for hotshots and 8% for box trucks, with no setup fee and no long-term contract. On a semi grossing $8,000 a week, 5% is $400.

A worked example (hypothetical)

Take a dry van semi grossing $8,000 a week on self-dispatch, with 18% deadhead.

Self-dispatch With a dispatcher
Weekly gross $8,000 $8,400 (better rates on 4 loads)
Dispatch fee $0 $420 (5%)
Empty miles 450 300
Fuel saved on 150 fewer empty miles (about 6.5 mpg, $3.70/gal) about $85
Hours on the phone per week about 12 about 2

In this example the fee is roughly covered by the higher rates and lower fuel cost, and the carrier gets about 10 hours a week back. Change the numbers and the answer changes. That is the point: run your own. The load profitability calculator and deadhead miles calculator help.

When self-dispatch makes sense

  • You already have steady direct freight or a dedicated lane.
  • You enjoy negotiating and have the time to do it well.
  • You run local or regional loads with the same customers every week.
  • Your margins are so tight that any fee breaks the budget, and you would rather spend the time.

When a dispatcher makes sense

  • You spend more time on the phone than you want to, or you are calling brokers while driving.
  • Your deadhead percentage is high and you keep getting stuck in weak markets.
  • You have new authority and need brokers who will set you up.
  • You run more than one truck and are dispatching at night.
  • Paperwork is slipping and invoices are going out late.

How to find high paying loads either way

Whether you self-dispatch or not, the same habits pay:

  1. Judge loads per total mile, including deadhead. A $3.20 load with 120 empty miles can pay less than a $2.90 load next door. See best paying loads per mile.
  2. Plan the reload before you accept the load. Check outbound freight in the delivery market.
  3. Negotiate every time. Ask for more, especially on short-notice and hard-to-cover loads. Our guide on rate negotiation with brokers has scripts.
  4. Build repeat lanes. Brokers rebook carriers who deliver on time and answer the phone.

A middle path

You do not have to choose all or nothing. Some carriers keep the board for their own repeat freight and use a dispatcher for the rest, or try a dispatcher for a few weeks and compare. With Texas Solutions your first load is dispatched free, so you can compare one real week. Talk to a dispatcher or read is a truck dispatcher worth it.

Frequently asked questions

Is self-dispatching cheaper than hiring a dispatcher?

On paper, yes: you keep the fee. In practice you pay with time, and sometimes with lower rates and more empty miles. Compare your weekly gross and deadhead percentage before and after, not just the fee.

Do dispatchers use the same load boards I can buy?

Usually yes, plus direct broker relationships. The difference is time and practice: a dispatcher searches and negotiates all day, knows which brokers rebook, and plans the next load before you deliver.

Can I use a dispatcher and still use my own load board?

Yes. Some carriers book their own repeat freight and use a dispatcher for the rest. Tell your dispatcher which loads you book yourself so you are not double-booked.

When does a dispatcher pay for itself?

When the extra rate per mile, the deadhead you avoid and the hours you get back are worth more than the fee. On a semi at 5%, a few dollars more per load and one less empty leg a week often covers it, but results vary.
