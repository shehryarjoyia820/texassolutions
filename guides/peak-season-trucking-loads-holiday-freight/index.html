Peak Season Trucking Loads: How Owner-Operators Can Plan Q4 Holiday Freight

By Texas Solutions Dispatch Team · October 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Finding loads Seasonal freight
Quick answer

How can owner-operators find more loads in peak season?

Q4 peak season brings retail, holiday food and e-commerce freight, mostly in October to December. Owner-operators do best by positioning near big distribution markets, booking reloads before they deliver, negotiating harder on short-notice freight and planning home time early, because rates and demand can swing quickly around Thanksgiving and Christmas.

The last three months of the year are the busiest stretch for many carriers. Peak season trucking loads come from retail restocking, holiday food and grocery, and e-commerce. For an owner-operator, Q4 can be the best quarter of the year or a frustrating one stuck in the wrong market. This guide shows how to plan holiday freight loads, which regions to watch, and how to keep deadhead down when everyone is busy.

What drives Q4 freight

  • Retail restocking. Stores and warehouses fill shelves from late summer through November.
  • Holiday food. Turkey, meat, dairy, baking goods and frozen food move hard before Thanksgiving and Christmas. Reefer demand climbs.
  • E-commerce. Parcel and fulfillment networks need extra capacity, which pulls dry van, power only and box truck freight.
  • Year-end shipping. Some shippers move freight before the year closes.

Then, in the last week or so of December, freight drops off as receivers close. Plan for both.

Reefer rates per mile in peak season

Reefer is usually the first trailer to feel the holiday rush. Our dispatchers plan reefer freight around roughly $4 to $6 a mile (October 2026 planning range, not a market index or quote). Reefer rates per mile today on your lane can sit above or below that. Check the board daily, and read our reefer loads guide for what to confirm before booking.

Regional picture: Texas, Midwest and California loads

Texas loads

Dallas-Fort Worth and Houston are national distribution hubs, and Laredo is the busiest cross-border crossing. Q4 brings retail, grocery and cross-border freight. Houston also stays busy with energy and construction freight for hotshots and flatbeds. See Texas truck dispatch.

Midwest loads

Chicago, Indianapolis, Columbus and Kansas City host large warehouse and rail networks. Midwest loads in Q4 include retail, food processing and the end of the harvest. Winter weather can slow everything, so build in time. See Illinois truck dispatch, Ohio and Indiana.

California loads

The Southern California ports and Inland Empire warehouses push import freight inland for the holidays. California loads often pay well outbound; the challenge is getting back in without a long empty run. See California truck dispatch.

For other states, use our truck dispatch by state list.

Seven ways to make the most of peak season

  1. Position early. Be near a strong outbound market before the rush, not after.
  2. Book the reload first. Before you accept a load, check freight out of the delivery city.
  3. Negotiate harder on short-notice loads. Brokers with uncovered holiday freight have more room.
  4. Watch deadhead. A high rate with 200 empty miles may pay less than a normal load next door. Use the deadhead miles calculator.
  5. Plan for waiting. Warehouses are busy; log check-in times and claim detention. Try the detention pay calculator.
  6. Keep the truck ready. A breakdown in December costs more than any rate gain. Budget with the maintenance calculator.
  7. Set home time now. Pick your dates and plan the last loads so you end near home.

Watch your hours

Peak season tempts long days. The hours-of-service limits do not change for the holidays. Plan loads you can run legally with the hours of service calculator.

How a dispatcher helps in Q4

Frequently asked questions

When is peak season in trucking?

Retail freight builds from late summer as stores stock up, then holiday food, grocery and e-commerce freight peaks from November through the week before Christmas. Produce seasons also create regional peaks earlier in the year.

Do rates go up in peak season?

Often, especially for reefer and short-notice loads, but not on every lane and not every year. Check lane rates daily and negotiate. Nobody can guarantee peak rates.

Where are the best holiday freight loads?

Around large distribution and port markets: Southern California, Dallas-Fort Worth and Houston, Chicago and the Midwest, Atlanta, and the Northeast corridor. The key is booking out of these markets, not only into them.

Should I take time off during the holidays?

Plan it early. Freight slows sharply in the last days of December and many receivers close. Tell your dispatcher your home-time dates so the last load of the year ends near home.
