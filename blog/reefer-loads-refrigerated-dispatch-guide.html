Reefer Loads and Refrigerated Load Dispatch: Rates per Mile, Seasons and Tips

By Texas Solutions Dispatch Team · October 21, 2026 · 2 min read

Reefer freight pays more than dry van for a reason: it is harder to haul. A missed appointment or a wrong temperature can mean a rejected load and a claim. Good refrigerated load dispatch is about the details. This guide covers reefer rates per mile, the seasons that drive reefer freight, and what to check before you book.

Reefer rates per mile in 2026

Our dispatchers plan reefer freight at roughly $4 to $6 a mile (October 2026). That is a planning range, not a market index or a quote. Real reefer rates per mile today depend on:

  • Lane and direction. Loads out of produce and food markets in season pay better than loads into them.
  • Distance. Short loads often pay more per mile; long runs pay more per load.
  • Season. Produce season and the holiday peak lift reefer demand.
  • Appointment pressure. Short-notice or hard-to-cover loads leave more room to negotiate.

Always compare loads by rate per total mile, including the empty miles to pick up. The load profitability calculator lets you count reefer fuel too.

The reefer calendar

Months Where reefer freight builds
March to May Florida, South Texas and Mexico border crossings (produce)
May to August California, Arizona, the Northwest
August to October Midwest and Northeast harvests, Michigan, Wisconsin, upstate New York
November to December Holiday peak season: grocery, meat, dairy and frozen food distribution nationwide

Planning lanes around this calendar keeps a reefer loaded both ways. See peak season trucking loads for the Q4 picture.

What to check before you accept a reefer load

  1. Temperature setting and whether the unit must run continuous or cycle.
  2. Pre-cool requirements before arrival at the shipper.
  3. Appointments: pickup and delivery times, and what happens if you are late.
  4. Pulp temperature rules for produce, and who checks it.
  5. Weight, so you stay legal with fuel in both tanks.
  6. Temperature download or seal requirements at delivery.
  7. Lumper fees at grocery warehouses, and how they are paid back. The detention pay calculator helps you claim waiting time.

Reefer fuel: the hidden cost

A reefer unit burns diesel even when the truck is parked, often somewhere around a gallon an hour depending on the unit, setting and outside temperature. On a long load with waiting time, that is real money. Add it to your truck fuel cost when you price a load.

How to find better reefer loads

  • Book the reload before you deliver. Grocery distribution centers often sit in markets with weak outbound freight.
  • Follow the season. Position your truck where produce is moving next.
  • Build broker relationships. Reefer brokers rebook carriers who hit appointments and keep temperatures right.
  • Negotiate. Ask for more on short-notice loads and loads with long waits.

Frequently asked questions

What are reefer rates per mile today?

Reefer rates change daily by lane. As a planning range our dispatchers use roughly $4 to $6 a mile in October 2026. Check current lane rates on your load board before you accept a load, and judge every load by its rate per total mile including deadhead.

Why do reefer loads pay more than dry van?

Reefer freight costs more to haul: fuel for the reefer unit, tighter appointments, temperature rules and the risk of a rejected load. Brokers pay more to cover that.

When is reefer peak season?

Produce moves from Florida, Texas and Mexico crossings in spring, through California and the Northwest in summer, and the Midwest and Northeast in late summer and fall. November and December add the holiday food and grocery peak.

What does a reefer dispatch service check before booking?

Temperature setting, continuous or cycle mode, pickup and delivery appointments, pulp temperature rules, load weight, and whether the shipper needs a temperature download. Texas Solutions confirms these with the broker before a load reaches you.
