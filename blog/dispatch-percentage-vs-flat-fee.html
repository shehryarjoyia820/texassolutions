Dispatch Service Percentage vs Flat Fee: Which Is Better?

By Texas Solutions Dispatch Team · October 13, 2026 · 4 min read

Dispatch pricing Owner-operators
Quick answer

Is a percentage or a flat fee better for truck dispatch?

A percentage fee rises and falls with what your truck earns, so you pay less in slow weeks and the dispatcher is motivated to book better-paying freight. A flat fee is predictable but costs the same in a bad week. The right choice depends on your weekly gross, how steady your freight is, and whether the offer hides extra charges or contracts.

When owner-operators compare dispatch services, the first question is usually about price. The second is about how the price is charged. This guide compares a dispatch service percentage vs flat fee, shows the math on both, and explains how to tell an affordable truck dispatch service from one that is only cheap on the surface.

The two common pricing models

Percentage of gross

The dispatcher charges a set percentage of what the truck earns, usually per week.

  • Most of the market sits roughly between 3% and 10%
  • Semi truck dispatch tends to be at the lower end
  • Box truck and hotshot dispatch tend to be higher, because smaller loads take more calls and paperwork per dollar

Flat fee

The dispatcher charges a fixed amount per truck per week or per month, no matter how much the truck grosses.

Some services also charge per load, which behaves like a flat fee on each booking.

Pros and cons of each

Percentage fee

Pros:

  • You pay less in slow weeks or when the truck is in the shop
  • The dispatcher earns more only when you earn more
  • Easy to compare across services

Cons:

  • In a very strong week, the fee is higher in dollars
  • You need to know exactly what the percentage applies to (line haul only, or including accessorials like detention)

Flat fee

Pros:

  • Predictable cost
  • In very high-grossing weeks, the fee can work out cheaper as a percentage

Cons:

  • You pay the same in a week when the truck sits
  • Less built-in reason for the dispatcher to chase a higher rate, since their pay does not change
  • Can cost more than a percentage for low-gross equipment

Worked example: same truck, three different weeks

Here is a semi on a 5% fee compared with a hypothetical flat fee of $450 a week. The flat figure is only an assumption for the comparison, not any particular company's price.

Week Weekly gross 5% fee Hypothetical flat fee
Slow week $5,000 $5,000 x 0.05 = $250 $450
Average week $9,000 $9,000 x 0.05 = $450 $450
Strong week $11,000 $11,000 x 0.05 = $550 $450

Over these three weeks:

  • Percentage total: $250 + $450 + $550 = $1,250
  • Flat fee total: $450 x 3 = $1,350

In this example the percentage costs less overall, because the slow week saves more than the strong week costs. Change the assumptions and the result changes. If your truck grosses well above average every week, a flat fee could come out ahead. If your weeks swing up and down, the percentage usually protects you more. Watch out for "cheap" dispatch offers

A low headline price can hide charges that make it the most expensive option. Before you sign with a cheap truck dispatch service, ask about:

  • Setup or onboarding fees charged before any load is booked
  • Per-packet or per-broker fees for carrier setup paperwork
  • Charges on the full invoice, including detention, lumper reimbursements and fuel surcharge, when you thought the fee was on line haul only
  • Minimum weekly charges that apply even when the truck sits
  • Long contracts with cancellation or early termination fees
  • Required factoring companies you are pushed to use, sometimes with terms that do not favor you
  • Access to your bank or factoring account without clear limits

A truly affordable dispatch service tells you the full cost up front and puts it in writing.

How to compare two offers fairly

  1. Write down your expected weekly gross for a slow, average and strong week
  2. Calculate each offer's cost in all three weeks
  3. Add every extra fee from the agreement
  4. Check contract length and how to cancel
  5. Ask whether you approve every load

What really decides the value

The fee is only half of the picture. A dispatcher who costs a little more but books better-paying loads with less deadhead can leave you with more money than a cheaper one who takes whatever is posted. Check every load on its own numbers with the load profitability calculator. No dispatcher can guarantee rates or earnings, so judge a service on how it works, how clearly it charges, and whether the math holds up on your truck.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage do truck dispatchers charge?

Most independent dispatchers charge somewhere between 3% and 10% of the truck's gross. Texas Solutions charges 5% for semi trucks, 7% for hotshots and 8% for box trucks and straight trucks running OTR.

Does Texas Solutions offer a flat rate?

No. Texas Solutions charges a percentage of weekly gross only. There is no flat rate, no setup fee and no long-term contract.

Is a cheap truck dispatch service worth it?

Only if the low price is the real price. Check for setup fees, per-packet charges, cancellation fees and long contracts, and ask exactly what the fee is calculated on before you sign.

Can I use a dispatch service with no contract?

Yes. Many dispatch services, including Texas Solutions, work without a long-term contract. You should still have a written dispatch agreement that states the fee and what it covers.
