How to Choose a Fuel Card for Truckers: What to Compare

By Texas Solutions Dispatch Team · October 13, 2026 · 6 min read

Fuel Owner-operators
Quick answer

How do I choose the best fuel card for truckers?

Start with where you actually fuel, then compare how the discount is calculated (retail-minus or cost-plus), which truck stops accept the card, every fee, whether it is credit or prepaid, and how well it reports gallons by state for IFTA. The best fuel card for truckers is the one that fits your lanes and paperwork, not the one with the biggest advertised number.

Fuel is usually the biggest bill an owner-operator pays that changes every week. A fuel card will not change the price of diesel, but the right one can trim what you pay per gallon, cut down on paperwork and make tax time less painful.

There is no single best fuel card for truckers. Truck stop chains, fleet card companies and factoring companies all offer cards, and each is built around a different kind of customer. This guide explains how to compare them so you can pick one that fits the way you run.

You will find plenty of lists of the best fuel cards for truckers online. We do not rank brands here. Discounts, fees and terms change often, and you should verify them directly with any provider before signing up.

Step 1: Know where you fuel today

Before you look at any offer, pull your last two or three months of fuel receipts. Note:

  • Which truck stop chains and independent stations you use most
  • The states you fuel in, and which lanes you run regularly
  • About how many gallons you buy each month
  • Whether you ever fuel at cardlock or unattended sites

A card with a strong discount at stations you never pass is worth very little. A card that works where you already stop is worth a lot more. If you are not sure what fuel costs on your lanes, check our diesel prices by state pages, which exist for each state.

Step 2: Understand how the discount works

This is the part that confuses most drivers. Fuel card discounts usually come in two forms.

Retail-minus

You pay the posted pump price minus a set amount per gallon. It is easy to understand and easy to check against the sign. The discount may be different from one chain or location to another.

Cost-plus

The price starts from what the station pays for fuel, then a markup is added. On some days and at some stations, this can come in well below the pump price. On others the gap is small. Cost-plus is harder to check because you cannot see the station's cost.

Other forms

Some cards offer rebates paid later, points or rewards at certain chains, or discounts only at an in-network list of locations. Ask exactly when and how any rebate is paid.

The only reliable way to compare is to look at what you would have paid on real fuel stops along your lanes, not at the headline number.

Step 3: Check network coverage

The best trucking fuel cards for one driver may be useless for another, mostly because of coverage. Ask:

  • Which truck stop chains accept the card, and is the discount the same at each?
  • Does it work at independent stations, and is there any discount there?
  • Can you buy DEF, reefer fuel or other items on it?
  • Does it work in every state you run?

If you run regional lanes, a card tied to one chain might be fine. If your lanes change week to week, broad acceptance matters more.

Step 4: Add up every fee

A discount can disappear fast once fees are counted. Look for:

  • Monthly or annual card fees
  • Per-transaction fees
  • Fees for using out-of-network stations
  • Late payment charges and interest on credit cards
  • Fees for adding cards or for paper statements

Ask the provider for a full fee schedule in writing. If they cannot give you one, keep looking.

Step 5: Credit or prepaid

Credit cards give you a line to fuel on now and pay later. That helps cash flow between loads, but usually requires a credit check or business history, and carrying a balance can cost you.

Prepaid or debit-style cards use money you load ahead of time. They can be easier to get for a new authority and help prevent overspending, but you need to keep the balance funded.

Cards through factoring companies are another option. Some factoring companies let you fuel against your advances. That can be convenient, but read how fuel purchases are deducted and whether the card ties you more tightly to that factor. Our guide to freight factoring explains how factoring works.

Step 6: IFTA reporting and fuel tax data

For an interstate carrier, a good fuel card for truckers is also a record-keeping tool. Look for:

  • Transaction reports that show gallons, price and state for every purchase
  • Downloadable reports by quarter
  • Easy matching between fuel records and your miles by state

Clean fuel data makes filing much smoother. See our guide to IFTA for owner-operators, or use the IFTA mileage calculator to work out miles by state.

Step 7: Controls and security

Even a one-truck operation benefits from basic controls, and small fleets need them more. Useful features include:

  • Purchase limits per day or per transaction
  • Restricting purchases to fuel only
  • Driver ID or PIN at the pump
  • Alerts for unusual transactions
  • A simple way to freeze a lost card

Worked example: how a per-gallon discount adds up

Here is how a discount adds up over time. These are assumptions for illustration only, not a quote from any card:

  • Fuel economy: 6.3 miles per gallon
  • Miles driven: 10,000 per month
  • Hypothetical discount: 20 cents per gallon

Math:

  1. Gallons per month: 10,000 / 6.3 = about 1,587 gallons
  2. Monthly discount: 1,587 x $0.20 = about $317
  3. Yearly discount: $317 x 12 = about $3,810

Now subtract any card fees. If the card cost, say, $15 a month in fees, the yearly benefit drops by $180. Also remember that a discount only helps if you actually fuel in network. Driving out of route to reach a discount station can cost more in fuel and time than it saves.

Your numbers will differ with mpg, miles, discount and fees. Plug your own figures into the truck fuel cost calculator, and see how fuel fits into your total costs with the cost per mile calculator.

A short checklist before you apply

  • Does it work where I fuel today?
  • Is the discount retail-minus, cost-plus or a rebate, and how will I verify it?
  • What is the full fee schedule?
  • Credit, prepaid or factoring-linked, and what are the approval terms?
  • Does it give clean state-by-state data for IFTA?
  • Can I set limits and freeze a card quickly?
  • Can I cancel without a penalty?

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between retail-minus and cost-plus fuel discounts?

A retail-minus discount takes a fixed amount off the posted pump price. A cost-plus price starts from the station's cost of the fuel and adds a markup. Which one saves more depends on the stations and the day, so compare real receipts on your own lanes.

Can a new owner-operator get a fuel card?

Often, yes. Some cards require a credit check or business history, while prepaid cards and cards offered through factoring companies may be easier to get for a new authority. Read the approval terms and fees before applying.

Does a fuel card help with IFTA?

It can. Many cards record gallons, price and location by state, which makes the fuel side of your quarterly IFTA return easier. You still need accurate miles by state, and receipts should be kept.
